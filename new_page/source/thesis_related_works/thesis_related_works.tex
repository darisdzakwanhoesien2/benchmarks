\subsection{2.1 ESG and Sustainability Reporting Context}
\subsubsection{2.1 Indonesian Sustainability Reporting Landscape: Regulatory Evolution and Empirical Compliance}

The Indonesian sustainability reporting landscape is characterized by a multi-phase transition from voluntary practices to institutionalized mandates. Firmansyah \& Wibowo  \parencite{amrie_firmansyah_200ee100} delineate this evolution across three distinct phases: the initiation phase marked by the issuance of POJK 51/2017, a transition period encompassing the \textit{Roadmap Keuangan Berkelanjutan Tahap II} (2021–2025), and a current phase of harmonization with global standards, specifically the adoption of IFRS S1 and S2 through the \textit{Pernyataan Standar Pengungkapan Keberlanjutan} 1 and 2  \parencite{amrie_firmansyah_200ee100}. This shift reflects a broader paradigmatic move from philanthropic-oriented CSR to strategic ESG integration, a trend confirmed by bibliometric analyses of Indonesian CSR scholarship  \parencite{boubacar_demba_barry_a607dad2}. Early research by Harymawan et al.  \parencite{iman_harymawan_2ef898fe} established the foundational understanding of this landscape (2013–2017), revealing generally low disclosure levels and an absence of universal guidance prior to the formal rollout of OJK regulations. While POJK 51/2017 provided the initial regulatory push for standardized disclosure  \parencite[]{alfonsa_roselina_purnomo_d67da232, kuat_waluyo_jati_45c636dc}, scholars have critiqued the mandate for lacking specific qualitative benchmarks for accuracy, balance, and reliability, which has perpetuated a "transparency gap" in local reporting  \parencite{atika_atika_588ee68a}.

Empirical assessments of sustainability reporting quality in Indonesia highlight persistent challenges in both readability and substance. Adhariani \& Toit  \parencite{desi_adhariani_toit_543335d6} identified a "low level of readability" across reports, observing a pattern of isomorphism where firms adopt homogenous linguistic structures that obscure critical information. Further, sector-specific studies suggest that companies prioritize certification disclosures—such as ISO or PROPER—over substantive operational impacts like pollution  \parencite{indah_fajarini_sri_wahyuningrum_1ce983fb}. For instance, among manufacturing and mining firms, the average environmental disclosure score stands at 54.94\%, reflecting a cautious approach to transparency  \parencite{indah_fajarini_sri_wahyuningrum_1ce983fb}. Compliance heterogeneity remains another significant issue; Situngdong Kombong \& Kurniawati  \parencite{christine_bawaeda_sitandung_kombong_d03ec902} found that while high-impact sectors like agriculture and aquaculture show awareness, approximately 40\% of companies remain in the "medium" implementation category for POJK 51 standards  \parencite{christine_bawaeda_sitandung_kombong_d03ec902}.

The technical complexity of this landscape is further complicated by the bilingual nature of disclosures, with nearly 100\% of public companies preparing reports in both Indonesian and English to meet regulatory provisions  \parencite{tri_siwi_nugrahani_056a6914}. This necessitates cross-lingual Natural Language Processing approaches to manage information extraction. Moreover, as professional practices shift toward IFRS-aligned standards, practitioners face a distinct "competency gap" and infrastructure limitations  \parencite{amrie_firmansyah_fd5249a2}. Recent computational approaches, such as the application of DistilBERT to classify ESG segments and detect greenwashing, have begun to address these bottlenecks by identifying that while 39.1\% of FMCG company reports show fair disclosure, 18.5\% exhibit strong greenwashing tendencies  \parencite{anisa_kusuma_putri_4109a23b}. The field has increasingly pivoted toward systematic literature reviews to map these trends in diversity, firm value, and sustainability reporting  \parencite{irma_hidayati_373b816a}, alongside longitudinal observations that indicate a trend toward annual reporting at the expense of integrated report formats  \parencite{kostiantyn_bezverkhyi_e3819b1b}.

\subsection{2.2 Automated ESG Information Extraction and Classification}

The research landscape for automated Environmental, Social, and Governance information extraction has evolved from rudimentary keyword-matching and dictionary-based approaches to sophisticated agentic Large Language Model pipelines. Anaraki et al.  \parencite{seyed_alireza_mousavian_anaraki_b56a1c0f} provide a foundational taxonomy of these developments, noting that the unstructured and heterogeneous nature of corporate sustainability reports poses significant challenges for automated classification. The shift toward LLM-driven frameworks has enabled more granular, schema-aware analysis, effectively moving beyond surface-level sentiment to extract structured data compatible with regulatory reporting standards such as GRI and SASB  \parencite{seyed_alireza_mousavian_anaraki_b56a1c0f}.

Central to this progress is the use of the Retrieval-Augmented Generation paradigm to ground extraction processes. Bronzini et al.  \parencite[]{marco_bronzini_bf94d949, marco_bronzini_bf94d949} demonstrated that LLMs, when integrated with RAG and In-Context Learning, can derive structured JSON, tabular data, and ESG-oriented triples from unstructured narratives with high precision. Building upon this, Zou et al.  \parencite{yi_zou_37c055f7} developed the ESGReveal system, an agentic approach that utilizes an LLM agent to achieve 76.9\% accuracy in data extraction across 166 companies. This agentic paradigm facilitates complex information retrieval and disclosure analysis (achieving 83.7\% accuracy) that surpasses the capabilities of traditional Open Information Extraction methods  \parencite[]{yi_zou_37c055f7, marco_bronzini_bf94d949}.

Recent advancements have focused on structural robustness and domain compliance to bridge the gap between extraction and standard-aligned reporting. EulerESG, developed by Ding et al.  \parencite{yi_ding_136f177b}, addresses the limitations of brittle, rule-based extraction by utilizing a dual-channel retrieval system to populate standard-aligned metric tables with up to 0.95 average accuracy. To manage the "black-box" risks of LLMs in risk-averse financial domains, Weichel et al.  \parencite{hendrik_weichel_5ea70935} proposed a two-step hybrid methodology combining rule-based table extraction with regularized LLMs. This approach optimizes for robustness in parsing complex financial and emissions tables while minimizing false values  \parencite{hendrik_weichel_5ea70935}.

Furthermore, the integration of framework-aware verification modules is essential for ensuring that extracted data is auditable. Gupta et al.  \parencite{tanay_gupta_b5c91517} introduced LLM-based modules designed to assess the compliance of sustainability disclosures against established frameworks, identifying significant gaps in sector-specific reporting quality. In the context of emerging markets, Fan et al.  \parencite{j__h__fan_97bd591d} presented the FinATG framework, which utilizes domain-adaptive span representations and constrained decoding to achieve an entity F1 score of 88.5 for extracting carbon and green investment data. Finally, to counteract greenwashing risks, Ong et al.  \parencite[]{keane_ong_369a0b63, keane_ong_369a0b63} introduced Aspect-Action Analysis with Cross-Category Generalization (A3CG). This semantic logic explicitly links broad environmental claims ("Aspects") to verifiable corporate investments ("Actions"), ensuring that the generated data reflects objective performance rather than strategic ambiguity  \parencite[]{keane_ong_369a0b63, keane_ong_369a0b63}.

\subsection{2.3 ESG Sentiment and Aspect-Based Analysis}

The field of Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis for Environmental, Social, and Governance domains has evolved from traditional sentence-level classification to sophisticated hierarchical, neurosymbolic, and cross-lingual architectures. Early limitations in sentiment analysis arose because standard models often failed to address the nuance of multiple targets within a single sentence  \parencite{blaise_sandwidi_c58b16e0}. To resolve this, researchers introduced the Sustainability Term-Based Sentiment Analysis method, which leverages attention-based neural networks to focus on specific sustainability terms, achieving an accuracy of 91.30\% and a 90\% F1-score on a dataset of over 125,000 annotated data points  \parencite{blaise_sandwidi_c58b16e0}.
\subsubsection{Hierarchical and Neurosymbolic Architectures}

Current methodologies prioritize the hierarchical structure of ESG data to improve prediction granularity. Lengkeek et al.  \parencite{matteo_lengkeek_5d9f2a4e} developed a two-step structure that utilizes parent-aspect predictions to refine child-aspect polarity, resulting in a 7.6\% increase in F1-scores on financial datasets  \parencite{matteo_lengkeek_5d9f2a4e}. Moving beyond purely neural approaches, neurosymbolic AI has emerged as a robust solution for mining key aspects of socially responsible investing by combining neural pattern recognition with symbolic reasoning  \parencite{wihan_van_der_heever_5ff35709}. For instance, Ong et al.  \parencite{keane_ong_8fec4760} introduced ESGSenticNet, a neurosymbolic knowledge base constructed from 1,679 sustainability reports that integrates symbolic parsing rules with LLM-based labeling to enforce alignment with hierarchical ESG taxonomies  \parencite{keane_ong_8fec4760}.

To bridge the gap between regulatory standards and computational extraction, several studies utilize formal ontologies to guide models. Usmanova \& Usbeck  \parencite{aida_usmanova_e9b44e1d} demonstrated that LLMs guided by domain-specific ontologies can effectively model ESRS topical standards and minimize hallucinations during report analysis  \parencite{aida_usmanova_e9b44e1d}. Similarly, Iwata et al.  \parencite{tsuyoshi_iwata_18ccc0b8} employed a semi-automatic ontology construction method to map news-reported ESG controversies to normative frameworks like the SDGs, ensuring high-quality, interpretable knowledge graphs  \parencite{tsuyoshi_iwata_18ccc0b8}. This alignment with compliance standards is further reflected in Naskar et al.  \parencite{abir_naskar_17efeb7b}, who utilized a multi-tasking sequence labeling architecture based on Global Reporting Initiative standards to detect organizational violations  \parencite{abir_naskar_17efeb7b}.
\subsubsection{Regional and Cross-Lingual Trends}

In regional contexts, research has focused on adapting these models to localized data. In Indonesia, Prathama et al.  \parencite{ubaidillah_ariq_prathama_a1856e2f} integrated BERT-based aspect classification with generative LLMs and Bi-LSTM to predict stock prices, achieving a 31.11\% reduction in RMSE  \parencite{ubaidillah_ariq_prathama_a1856e2f}. Meanwhile, Octovianto et al.  \parencite{christofer_octovianto_487e90aa} established a benchmark for Indonesian environmental sentiment analysis on social media, identifying the IndoBERT-large-p2 model as achieving a 72.44\% F1-score  \parencite{christofer_octovianto_487e90aa}.

For global applications, cross-lingual collaboration has become critical to overcoming the scarcity of high-resource language data. Zhang et al.  \parencite{liman_zhang_ec332205} proposed a framework that integrates semantic contrastive learning with language-adaptive modulation, achieving 75.8\% accuracy in trilingual financial sentiment recognition  \parencite{liman_zhang_ec332205}. Expanding on this, He et al.  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_5a25f994} introduced a Cross-Lingual Collaboration method that aligns semantic and syntactic structures across languages to enhance zero-shot entity-level sentiment analysis  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_5a25f994}. Finally, Ibn Ahad et al.  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_1ba46dee} addressed multilingual decision-making by implementing a bidirectional reasoning supervision approach, which incorporates both positive and negative rationales to improve classification performance across low, medium, and high-resource languages  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_1ba46dee}.

\subsection{2.4 Greenwashing Detection and ESG Verification}

The research landscape for mitigating greenwashing and verifying Environmental, Social, and Governance disclosures has rapidly evolved from simple text classification to sophisticated, agentic architectures. Current literature focuses on three primary pillars: the development of domain-specific benchmarks, the application of hallucination mitigation strategies, and the design of robust, evidence-based verification frameworks.
\subsubsection{Benchmark Development and Data-Driven Evaluation}

To address the scarcity of high-quality data for training and evaluating models in sustainability domains, several researchers have introduced specialized benchmarks. Zhao et al.  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8} developed a three-level hierarchical benchmark derived from 310 corporate sustainability reports, designed to test capabilities ranging from simple factual extraction to complex, multi-step auditing. Similarly, Bai et al.  \parencite{association_for_artificial_intelligence_2026_86e12eae} introduced ESG-Bench, which utilizes human-annotated question-answer pairs grounded in real-world contexts to evaluate Large Language Model reasoning and factuality in compliance-critical environments. On a more granular level, Birti et al.  \parencite{mattia_birti_c097e3e8} addressed the need for specific activity detection by constructing the ESG-Activities dataset, which comprises 1,325 labeled text segments mapped to the EU ESG taxonomy. These benchmarks demonstrate that fine-tuning on domain-specific data significantly improves classification accuracy compared to general-purpose prompting  \parencite{mattia_birti_c097e3e8}.
\subsubsection{Hallucination Mitigation and Uncertainty Estimation}

Addressing the propensity of LLMs to hallucinate in compliance-critical environments is a focal point of recent research. Wu et al.  \parencite{yue_wu_cae353aa} demonstrated the efficacy of a Chain-of-Verification method, which successfully reduced hallucination rates from 16\% down to 10\% in sustainability report scoring. Broader techniques for addressing these reliability gaps include logit-based uncertainty detection and Pareto optimal self-supervision, which allow systems to estimate the risk of erroneous outputs  \parencite{yue_zhang_9101e661}. In high-stakes financial advising, Jasem  \parencite{al_noor_ali_aziz_jasem_af362154} proposes a framework utilizing semantic entropy-based uncertainty gating to trigger validation efforts dynamically and prevent unsupported claims. Similarly, Sood  \parencite{rohit_sood_cb3a4ead} advocates for human–AI co-governance models within enterprise metaverse environments, where explainability, stress-testing, and escalation protocols serve to detect and constrain hallucinations in real-time.
\subsubsection{Verification Frameworks and Agentic Architectures}

Recent frameworks have shifted from simple text generation to evidence-backed, transparent verification. Aguirre Calderón  \parencite{juan_aguirre_calder_n_5eae82e0} implemented a Retrieval-Augmented Generation approach that integrates a post-generation verification layer to ensure citation traceability, achieving an 80\% reduction in manual analyst labor. Similar frameworks prioritize structure and accountability; for instance, AuditGPT uses RAG-based vector stores to generate structured verdicts—categorized as Compliant, Non-Compliant, or Requires Review—supported by traceable references  \parencite{abhijeet_b__moghe_963e6c96}.

Kaoukis et al.  \parencite{kaoukis__georgios_35bab107} introduced EmeraldMind, a framework that integrates a domain-specific knowledge graph to surface verifiable evidence and allow the model to "abstain responsibly" when claims cannot be confirmed. Taking a different approach to reliability, Nohr  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924} contrasts probabilistic LLM ratings with deterministic rule-layer systems like the Crystal Validator, which enforces full-lifecycle provenance to generate cryptographically verifiable evidence for institutional-grade tokenized assets, ensuring auditability that standard ratings cannot achieve.

\subsection{2.5 LLM with Zero-shot, Few-shot and Chain-of-thought in Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis}

The landscape of Aspect-based Sentiment Analysis has undergone a significant paradigm shift, moving from supervised fine-tuning of Pre-trained Language Models to generative paradigms leveraging Large Language Models  \parencite[]{chengyan_wu_4c659eab, zhiyuan_ma_3b2e6db4}. While early methods relied on sequence tagging and auxiliary subtasks to identify aspect terms and sentiment polarity, contemporary research focuses on the adaptability and in-context learning capabilities of LLMs to overcome data scarcity and cross-domain generalization challenges  \parencite[]{nils_constantin_hellwig_6fa4e097, jakob_fehle_a0e62c9d}.
\subsubsection{In-Context Learning and Domain-Specific Adaptation}

Zero-shot and few-shot in-context learning have emerged as efficient alternatives to the resource-intensive process of fine-tuning. Guo et al.  \parencite{yue_guo_642efc19} established a foundational evaluation framework for financial NLP, demonstrating that decoder-only models can perform aspect-based financial sentiment analysis using manual zero-shot and few-shot prompts. In subsequent evaluations, Guo et al.  \parencite{yue_guo_642efc19} utilized nine financial datasets to show that example selection—whether random or based on similarity—significantly impacts ChatGPT's performance.

To maximize the utility of these models, researchers have focused on self-adaptive instruction optimization and data augmentation. Jin et al.  \parencite{weiqiang_jin_5ccfa531} proposed the BYD-OBS-ABSA framework, which employs a random search bootstrap algorithm to iteratively refine instructions and mitigate LLM hallucinations. In low-resource settings, Hellwig et al.  \parencite{nils_constantin_hellwig_6fa4e097} investigated the use of few-shot prompting to generate synthetic training samples, significantly enhancing F1 scores for Aspect Category Detection.

The application of LLMs in specialized regional domains further highlights these capabilities. Prathama et al.  \parencite{ubaidillah_ariq_prathama_a1856e2f} utilized a hybrid approach where BERT handles aspect classification and generative LLMs manage sentiment for Indonesian news, resulting in a 31.11\% decrease in RMSE for stock price prediction. Similarly, Hirunsri and Nadee  \parencite{tharnsaithong_hirunsri_e4c4d75b} applied Transformers to Thai ESG annual reports, finding that domain-specific models achieve higher accuracy than general-purpose ones. Multilingual challenges are also addressed through bidirectional reasoning supervision by Ibn Ahad et al.  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_1ba46dee}, which integrates positive and negative rationales across English, Hindi, Bengali, and Telugu, while He et al.  \parencite{association_for_computational_linguistics_2025_5a25f994} introduced Cross-Lingual Collaboration to align semantic structures for zero-shot entity-level sentiment analysis.
\subsubsection{Chain-of-Thought and Reasoning Frameworks}

The limitation of standard prompt-based learning in handling complex, multi-opinion sentences has led to the development of reasoning-centric frameworks. Ventirozos et al.  \parencite{filippos_ventirozos_5976f1ad} introduced a technique using Unified Meaning Representation to structure reasoning, while Wu et al.  \parencite{chengyan_wu_4c659eab} highlighted that self-improvement and self-debate mechanisms significantly enhance transparency and accuracy in multi-step tasks. Wu et al.  \parencite{chengyan_wu_4c659eab} also found that while LLMs show promise in multilingual ABSA, they often require sophisticated reasoning paths to match task-specific models.

To address the nuances of implicit sentiment analysis, where sentiment indicators are not explicit, researchers have introduced progressive and syntactic strategies. Liu et al.  \parencite{chang_liu_7bb298f7} proposed the Progressive Chain-of-thought framework, incorporating Tree-of-Thought and chain self-consistency to infer intensity and opinion reasons, achieving 86.12\% accuracy on the restaurant dataset  \parencite{chang_liu_7bb298f7}. Similarly, Radi et al.  \parencite{mohammad_radi_96ee5847} introduced the Iterative Syntactic-Guided Chain of Thought framework, which leverages dependency parsing to navigate multi-opinion contexts, achieving F1 scores of 80.43 and 84.47 on the Sem-Eval 2015 and 2016 datasets  \parencite{mohammad_radi_96ee5847}. Advancing these multi-dimensional paths, He et al.  \parencite{yating_he_0bd5c04f} developed the Multi-Chain Thought Prompt Learning framework, simulating human multi-path reasoning to deeply explore sentiment cues across diverse perspectives  \parencite[]{yating_he_0bd5c04f, filippos_ventirozos_5976f1ad}.

\subsection{2.6 Knowledge-Guided, Ontology-Based, and Neuro-Symbolic ESG Analysis}

The integration of structured domain knowledge with generative AI models has emerged as the definitive solution for overcoming the limitations of pure Large Language Models in Environmental, Social, and Governance analysis. While pure LLM approaches excel at pattern recognition, they are frequently constrained by "hallucinations"—the generation of factually incorrect or ungrounded outputs—as well as challenges related to "immateriality" (the extraction of topics lacking business value) and "semantic alignment" (the mismatch between corporate narratives and standardized reporting frameworks)  \parencite[]{arash_hajikhani_cbb2528f, keane_ong_8fec4760}. To address these, recent research has transitioned from simple keyword-based NLP to sophisticated, structure-aware architectures that handle the fragmentation of non-financial reporting  \parencite[]{annas_vijaya_dc57c7ec, chaoyue_he_8c389604}. Recent literature classifies this progression into two stages: Data-to-KG (knowledge generation) and KG-to-App (application integration), distilled into paradigms such as ontology-first lifting, rule-supervised extraction, and agentic validation pipelines  \parencite{chaoyue_he_8c389604}.
\subsubsection{Semantic Alignment and Ontology-Driven Reporting}

Ontology-based methods provide the structural backbone required to align unstructured disclosures with evolving international standards such as the ESRS and GRI. Usmanova and Usbeck  \parencite{aida_usmanova_e9b44e1d} demonstrated that by guiding LLMs with formal ontologies modeled after ESRS topical standards, organizations can identify reporting gaps and automate knowledge graph construction with higher reliability than unguided models. Similarly, Iwata et al.  \parencite{tsuyoshi_iwata_18ccc0b8} utilized semi-automatic ontology construction to map news-reported controversies to normative principles like the UN Global Compact, transforming abstract regulatory language into structured, reusable RDF templates. This semantic grounding ensures that ESG data is not only machine-readable but also contextually mapped to normative frameworks  \parencite{tsuyoshi_iwata_18ccc0b8}. Further research includes specialized architectures such as the RSO  \parencite{yuchen_zhou_b6beee03} and ESGMKG  \parencite{mingqin_yu_96a5b8af}, which facilitate interoperability between disparate reporting standards and ensure cross-framework consistency  \parencite{annas_vijaya_dc57c7ec}.
\subsubsection{Neuro-Symbolic Hybrid Architectures}

Neuro-symbolic AI combines the flexibility of neural models with the interpretability of symbolic reasoning, effectively resolving the "semantic gap" found in traditional statistical NLP. Ong et al.  \parencite{keane_ong_8fec4760} introduced ESGSenticNet, a framework that utilizes symbolic parsing rules to extract meaningful concepts from 1,679 sustainability reports, subsequently using GPT-4o for hierarchical labeling. This hybrid structure ensures that extracted topics remain grounded in an ESG taxonomy, demonstrating that structural grounding significantly outperforms isolated dictionary-based approaches in topic alignment  \parencite{keane_ong_8fec4760}. This philosophy is echoed by van der Heever et al.  \parencite{wihan_van_der_heever_5ff35709}, whose neuro-symbolic framework leverages SenticNet for concept parsing alongside deep learning, significantly improving the scalability and interpretability of ESG metric mining through pattern recognition and deductive reasoning.
\subsubsection{Verifiability, Agentic Workflows, and Deterministic Layering}

To transition from probabilistic sentiment estimation to institutional-grade auditing, recent architectures have integrated retrieval-augmented generation with knowledge graphs and multi-agent systems to mitigate hallucination. Kaoukis et al.  \parencite{kaoukis__georgios_35bab107} developed EmeraldMind, a framework that pairs a domain-specific KG with RAG to deliver justification-centric greenwashing detection. By providing evidence-backed verdicts and abstaining when verification is impossible, this method directly tackles the hallucination problem inherent in standard RAG systems. Complementing these tools, ESGAgent  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8} employs a hierarchical multi-agent framework to handle complex, multi-step auditing tasks, achieving 84.15\% accuracy on QA tasks across 310 corporate reports by integrating verifiable references and diverse data modalities, including charts and text  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8}. Finally, Nohr  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924} argues that for high-stakes environments like tokenized real-world assets, probabilistic LLMs must be augmented with deterministic rule-layers (e.g., Crystal Validator™) to enforce lifecycle provenance and cross-ledger auditability—capabilities that cannot be achieved by LLMs alone.

\subsection{2.7 Multimodal and Document-Aware Sustainability-Report Analysis}

The landscape of corporate sustainability analysis has undergone a fundamental transition from manual coding and keyword-based algorithms to advanced deep learning architectures capable of processing heterogeneous data  \parencite{sarah_chepkogei_sawe_a4abd9c5}. Traditional text-only models frequently struggle with the "non-machine-readable" nature of sustainability reports, which utilize complex visual rhetoric, including infographics, flowcharts, and high-density tables, to influence stakeholder perceptions  \parencite[]{bingjie_li_487d69fd, tanay_gupta_aafe0c6a}. Recent research efforts have shifted toward multimodal architectures that treat these visual elements as core evidence rather than auxiliary artifacts.

To address the limitations of standard retrieval systems, multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation frameworks have emerged, facilitating a 6.5\% improvement in precision over text-only baselines by integrating visual and layout data into the retrieval pipeline  \parencite{g_saipooja_m_n_488bfdd6}. Within this domain, Vision Language Models have demonstrated significant utility in extracting specific metrics, such as greenhouse gas emission-reduction targets, achieving F1 scores as high as 0.82 when combined text and image modalities are processed  \parencite{lars_wilhelmi_8d7e860b}. However, these models face persistent challenges with visually dense layouts and the tendency toward inference-based hallucinations  \parencite{lars_wilhelmi_8d7e860b}.

Techniques for document-aware retrieval have further evolved to provide evidence-based provenance. Systems now employ computer vision to crop relevant report screenshots, providing direct visual citations for answers to queries such as Scope 3 GHG emissions  \parencite{pulkit_parikh_19ae49ff}. This aligns with broader movements toward "open box" architectures, such as ClimateQA, which utilize question-answering methodologies to navigate hundreds of pages of climate disclosures  \parencite{alexandra_sasha_luccioni_f5a602b0}. To resolve the "black-box" limitations of large language models, hybrid approaches—such as combining rule-based table extraction with regularized LLMs—have proven effective at maintaining robustness in risk-averse financial environments  \parencite{hendrik_weichel_5ea70935}.

Furthermore, quantitative tools have been developed to measure visual "impression management," using computer vision to decompose reports into text, image, and color to operationalize abstract design concepts into computable metrics  \parencite{bingjie_li_487d69fd}. Recent advancements in agentic workflows, such as ESGAgent, have further pushed capabilities, achieving 84.15\% accuracy on complex tasks across 310 reports  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8}, while Chain-of-Verification methods have been shown to reduce hallucination rates in sustainability scoring from 16\% to 10\%  \parencite{yue_wu_cae353aa}.

\subsection{2.8 ESG Datasets, Benchmarks, and Evaluation Practices}

The recent proliferation of Large Language Models in the domain of Environmental, Social, and Governance analysis has necessitated a transition from general-purpose document processing to specialized, verifiable frameworks. Current literature is defined by three primary streams: the development of gold-standard benchmarks for domain-specific information extraction, the implementation of robust hallucination mitigation and verification strategies, and the design of advanced agentic and Retrieval-Augmented Generation architectures.
\subsubsection{ESG Benchmark Design and Data Gaps}

Early research addressed the fragmented nature of ESG data by creating high-quality, ground-truth benchmarks to define the standards that modern frameworks must emulate. Beck et al.  \parencite{jacob_beck_9548a833} addressed foundational data gaps by developing a gold-standard dataset for the extraction of Scope 1, 2, and 3 GHG emissions from 139 sustainability reports. In the realm of Question Answering, Sun et al.  \parencite{siqi_sun_f6a51152} and Bai et al.  \parencite{association_for_artificial_intelligence_2026_86e12eae} introduced ESG-Bench, which employs human-annotated QA pairs to evaluate long-context reasoning and mitigate hallucinations in compliance-critical settings. Similarly, George \& Saji  \parencite{s__s__george_saji_b30d4b3e} proposed ESGBench, an evaluation framework that requires models to generate supporting evidence chains for answers to improve traceability and factual consistency  \parencite{s__s__george_saji_b30d4b3e}.

Linguistic and thematic specialization further characterizes this stream. Lee et al.  \parencite{jaeyoung_lee_1894333c} provided the ESG-Kor dataset, consisting of 118,946 manually labeled sentences for Korean financial documents, while Birti et al.  \parencite{mattia_birti_c097e3e8} established ESG-Activities, a benchmark of 1,325 labeled segments aligned with the EU ESG taxonomy. For "outside-in" verification—the process of validating corporate claims against public evidence—Pavlova et al.  \parencite{mariya_pavlova_259be499} and Billert \& Conrad  \parencite{fabian_billert_a5fbb687} developed news-based corpora to verify report claims against real-world sustainability events. Addressing the specific challenge of greenwashing, Ong et al.  \parencite{keane_ong_0a5de439} introduced the A3CG framework, which emphasizes aspect-action analysis and cross-category generalization to ensure insights are grounded in verifiable actions rather than vague or misleading rhetoric.
\subsubsection{Hallucination Mitigation and Verification Frameworks}

As ESG evaluation enters high-stakes financial environments, research has shifted toward ensuring auditability and mitigating model errors. Wu et al.  \parencite{yue_wu_cae353aa} demonstrated that a Chain-of-Verification self-correction method could reduce initial hallucination rates from 16\% down to 10\% while increasing overall accuracy to 58\%. Other frameworks prioritize structural verification; for instance, Nohr  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924} contrasted probabilistic LLM ratings with deterministic rule-layer systems (such as the Crystal Validator), arguing that the latter are necessary to generate transaction-level, cryptographically verifiable evidence for tokenized real-world assets.

Advanced verification techniques include Kaoukis et al.’s  \parencite{kaoukis__georgios_35bab107} EmeraldMind, which utilizes a domain-specific knowledge graph to deliver justification-centric verdicts and evidence-backed classifications. Liu et al.  \parencite{liyuan_liu_91c36432} proposed Veri-Green, which integrates blockchain-based incentive mechanisms with machine learning and Vickrey Clarke Groves auctions to ensure that third-party assurance is truthful and computationally efficient. For real-time advisory, Sood  \parencite{rohit_sood_cb3a4ead} proposed a human-AI co-governance framework involving explainability and escalation protocols to interpret and constrain hallucinations. Uncertainty-based approaches are also prevalent; Jasem  \parencite{al_noor_ali_aziz_jasem_af362154} presented a paradigm for semantic entropy-based uncertainty gating to dynamically control verification efforts and prevent unsupported claims during high-stakes communication.
\subsubsection{Advanced Agentic and RAG Architectures}

State-of-the-art developments are currently centered on autonomous, agentic systems capable of end-to-end report management and "open box" transparency. Zhao et al.  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8} introduced ESGAgent, a hierarchical multi-agent system achieving 84.15\% accuracy on complex analysis tasks across 310 corporate reports. Similarly, Hoang et al.  \parencite{thong_hoang_d30a390b} developed an agentic ESG lifecycle framework that transforms static reporting into a dynamic governance system by integrating tasks such as report validation, multi-report comparison, and knowledge-base maintenance.

Yim et al.  \parencite{tik_yu_yim_951fa18a} and Aguirre Calderón  \parencite{juan_aguirre_calder_n_5eae82e0} utilized RAG architectures to build transparent assessment systems for TCFD disclosures and corporate profiling. Notably, the implementation by Aguirre Calderón  \parencite{juan_aguirre_calder_n_5eae82e0} resulted in an 80\% reduction in the labor required for analysts to produce final company profiles. Finally, to support global reporting needs, Mashkin \& Chersoni  \parencite{ivan_mashkin_fba24085} addressed multilingual requirements, showing that cross-lingual Transformer representations can provide robust ESG issue identification for both English and French financial documents.

\subsection{2.9 Research Gaps and Positioning of the Proposed Framework}



The synthesis of existing literature (Sections 2.1 through 2.8) reveals a critical divergence between current computational capabilities and the rigorous requirements of Environmental, Social, and Governance analysis. While Large Language Models and automated extraction techniques have advanced significantly, several distinct gaps remain, particularly regarding localized regulatory shifts, structural auditability, and multimodal verification. This section delineates these research voids and positions the proposed framework as an integrated technical solution.
\subsubsection{2.9.1 Indonesian Contextual and Regulatory Gaps}

Current research in the Indonesian sustainability landscape identifies a profound "transparency gap" resulting from the historical reliance on POJK 51/2017  \parencite{atika_atika_588ee68a}. While this regulation initiated mandatory disclosure, it lacked specific qualitative benchmarks for accuracy and reliability, leading to a "low level of readability" and a pattern of "isomorphism" where firms adopt homogenous, obscure linguistic structures  \parencite{desi_adhariani_toit_543335d6}. As Indonesia transitions toward IFRS-aligned national standards (PSPK 1 and 2), practitioners face a significant "competency gap" and infrastructure limitations that hinder the integration of financial and non-financial information  \parencite[]{amrie_firmansyah_fd5249a2, amrie_firmansyah_200ee100}. Furthermore, despite the fact that nearly 100\% of Indonesian public companies prepare reports in both Indonesian and English to meet regulatory provisions  \parencite{tri_siwi_nugrahani_056a6914}, there is a lack of integrated bilingual NLP frameworks that can simultaneously manage cross-lingual extraction and predict market impacts, such as stock price fluctuations  \parencite{ubaidillah_ariq_prathama_a1856e2f}.
\subsubsection{2.9.2 Technical, Architectural, and Semantic Gaps}

A primary technical limitation in existing ESG analysis is the "black-box" nature of standard probabilistic LLMs. In risk-averse financial domains, these models are often insufficient because they cannot generate the transaction-level, cryptographically verifiable evidence required for institutional-grade auditing  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924}. There is an urgent need for "deterministic rule-layer verification" to ensure full-lifecycle provenance  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924}. Furthermore, current NLP approaches frequently suffer from "irrelevance" and "immateriality," where extracted topics lack meaningful business value or alignment with standardized ESG taxonomies  \parencite{keane_ong_8fec4760}. Existing models also fail to robustly mitigate greenwashing because they do not explicitly link broad "Aspects" (sustainability claims) to verifiable "Actions" (corporate investments), allowing strategic ambiguity to persist  \parencite[]{keane_ong_0a5de439, keane_ong_369a0b63}. To resolve this, systems must move toward "justification-centric" outputs that provide evidence-backed verdicts and "abstain responsibly" when confirmation is impossible  \parencite{kaoukis__georgios_35bab107}.
\subsubsection{2.9.3 Multimodal and Visual Representation Gaps}

Corporate sustainability reports are inherently multimodal, utilizing "visually dense layouts"—including infographics, charts, and high-density tables—to perform "impression management" and influence stakeholder perceptions  \parencite[]{lars_wilhelmi_8d7e860b, bingjie_li_487d69fd}. Standard text-only retrieval systems frequently overlook or misinterpret these visual components, leading to "inference-based hallucinations" where the model generates factually unsupported claims  \parencite[]{g_saipooja_m_n_488bfdd6, lars_wilhelmi_8d7e860b}. While current Vision Language Models show promise, achieving F1 scores of approximately 0.82 in metric extraction, they still struggle with the complexity of heterogeneous page layouts  \parencite[]{tanay_gupta_aafe0c6a, lars_wilhelmi_8d7e860b}. There remains a void in frameworks that can objectively operationalize abstract design concepts into computable metrics while maintaining the provenance of visual evidence  \parencite{bingjie_li_487d69fd}.
\subsubsection{2.9.4 Positioning of the Proposed Framework}

The proposed framework is positioned to bridge these academic and technical voids by integrating three core pillars:
\begin{enumerate}
\item 

\textbf{Agentic Multi-Step Workflows:} To overcome the limitations of single-prompt models, the framework adopts a hierarchical multi-agent approach (similar to ESGAgent  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8}), which has demonstrated 84.15\% accuracy on complex auditing tasks by integrating specialized toolsets and verifiable references  \parencite{yilei_zhao_b7bbd9c8}.\item 

\textbf{Neurosymbolic and Deterministic Reasoning:} By combining the pattern recognition of neural models with the structured reasoning of symbolic AI  \parencite{wihan_van_der_heever_5ff35709}, the framework resolves the semantic gap and ensures that extracted topics remain grounded in standardized taxonomies like the ESRS or PSPK. This is further reinforced by a deterministic rule-layer to provide the audit-grade verification lacking in probabilistic systems  \parencite{steven_paul_nohr_a6874924}.\item 

\textbf{Multimodal RAG and Bilingual Integration:} To address visual impression management, the framework utilizes a multimodal Retrieval-Augmented Generation architecture, which can provide a 6.5\% improvement in precision over text-only baselines by treating graphs and tables as core evidence  \parencite{g_saipooja_m_n_488bfdd6}. This system is specifically designed for the Indonesian market, supporting bilingual processing to close the regional competency and transparency gaps  \parencite[]{amrie_firmansyah_fd5249a2, tri_siwi_nugrahani_056a6914}.
\end{enumerate}

By synthesizing these elements, the proposed framework transitions ESG analysis from a "black-box" estimation process into a dynamic, accountable, and adaptive system for sustainability governance  \parencite{thong_hoang_d30a390b}. Moreover, by adopting open, standardized communication protocols like OVON, the framework ensures that every decision-making step remains fully auditable, effectively mitigating the risks of hallucination and opaque reasoning inherent in closed-source alternatives  \parencite{diego_gosmar_0ee23d5f}.
