Chapter 6: Conclusion

As stated earlier, the purpose of the present thesis is the creation of an executable framework for evaluating the structure of the ESG aspects presented in Indonesian sustainability reports. Specifically, this thesis aims to create an end-to-end process for turning an Indonesian sustainability report (in PDF format) into auditable records containing information about each page's source, aspects mentioned on it, assigned ESG pillars, sentiment, and disclosure tone.

The findings suggest that the approach taken is technically feasible and practically meaningful. The sample size used in the empirical experiment comprised 23 reports with a total of roughly 5,512 pages resulting in 332 tone-bearing ESG records and 2,074 rows in total. At the record level, the developed schema was shown to work perfectly with all 52 aspects correctly aligned to ontology paths. Furthermore, the comparison to ClimateBERT commitment labels revealed a significant degree of congruence between labels generated with the two models (83.7% agreement, Cohen's kappa = 0.645).

In this thesis, the most important methodological choice is conceptualizing the whole approach not as one of building a single machine learning model but of constructing an executable research environment for conducting the evaluation. As the results confirm, there are several important strengths of the approach suggested. First, traceability was successfully achieved due to page-aware structuring and linking of extracted aspects. Secondly, modularity enabled easy introduction of different modules with particular functionalities, which significantly facilitated the implementation process. Lastly, diagnostics were made simple due to the use of the data dashboard. The weaknesses of the current design are as follows: the sensitivity to prompts and models in tone detection; the necessity to validate some assumptions by means of the pilot study and proxy comparison; and the incomplete automation, including such problems as missing tones and schema drift.

From the perspective of both academic practice and the wider discourse surrounding ESG text analytics, the contribution of the thesis can be described as twofold. On the one hand, this work represents a practical achievement: an auditable workflow for transforming reports into structured records and a demonstration of the feasibility of performing ESG tone detection in bilingual and low-resource conditions. On the other hand, it can serve as the basis for further benchmark development by helping to identify the key challenges of reliable and accurate analysis and to understand what should be improved. From this point of view, this thesis adds value to the discourse by highlighting the need for document engineering, provenance preservation, and thoughtful evaluation design in addition to progress in modeling techniques.

In conclusion, it needs to be stated that the current work cannot be regarded as either a fully-fledged benchmark study or a greenwashing adjudication tool. Instead, its principal value lies in demonstrating the feasibility of creating such tools through the execution of an appropriate workflow and in identifying the core problems of ESG benchmarking.
