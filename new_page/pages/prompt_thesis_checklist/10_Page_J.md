Part J: Required Output

Provide the assessment in the following order.

1. Executive Evaluation

Give a concise assessment of:

overall thesis quality;
strongest elements;
greatest grading risks;
whether the thesis currently appears submission-ready;
the three most urgent revisions.

Do not assign a final University of Oulu grade unless an official scoring rubric with grade boundaries has been supplied.

2. Critical and Major Issues

Provide a prioritized table:

| Rank | Location | Issue | Evidence | Why It Matters | Required Revision | Severity | Confidence | Effort |

3. Objective and Research-Question Traceability Matrix

Use the matrix defined in Part B1.

4. Contradiction and Consistency Register

List all confirmed and probable thesis-wide inconsistencies.

5. Scientific-Rigor Assessment

Organize the findings under:

research design;
data;
annotation or ground truth;
methods;
baselines;
experimental setup;
metrics;
statistical validity;
error analysis;
reproducibility;
validity and generalizability.
6. State-of-the-Art and Literature Assessment

Identify:

strengths;
missing research areas;
weak method justification;
unsupported novelty claims;
citation verification needs.
7. Discussion and Conclusion Assessment

Evaluate:

depth of interpretation;
comparison with prior work;
limitations;
research-question answers;
consistency with the abstract and introduction;
overclaims.
8. Ethics, Authorship, AI, and Copyright Audit

Include:

contribution-statement assessment;
AI disclosure assessment;
research-ethics assessment;
copyright and permission table.
9. Structure and Paragraph Audit

Include:

chapter-level structural issues;
subsection-balance issues;
high-priority paragraph problems;
proposed reorganizations.
10. Language, Terminology, and Presentation Audit

Include:

recurring linguistic problems;
terminology consistency table;
typography issues;
accessibility issues.
11. Citation and Reference Audit

Separate findings into:

missing citations;
misplaced citations;
incomplete references;
IEEE-style problems;
source-content verification required;
confirmed citation-content mismatches.
12. Numerical Verification

Provide all values that could be checked and identify values that remain unverifiable.

13. Independent-Performance Assessment

Assess how clearly the thesis demonstrates independent work and where the contribution statement should be strengthened.

14. Chapter-by-Chapter Revision Checklist

For each chapter, provide:

| Chapter | Keep | Revise | Add | Remove or Move | Priority |

Partial

16. Final Revision Plan

Organize actions into:

Immediate: validity, ethics, or submission-blocking issues

List all Critical issues.

Before submission: grade-affecting issues

List all Major issues.

Quality improvement

List Moderate issues.

Final proofreading

List Minor issues.

For every action, specify:

exact location;
required change;
whether new analysis or experimentation is required;
whether source verification is required;
estimated effort: Low, Medium, or High.
Review Constraints
Do not praise sections without explaining why they are effective.
Do not criticize sections without citing evidence.
Do not recommend adding experiments unless they are necessary to support a central claim; explain when narrowing the claim would be a more realistic alternative.
Do not confuse implementation complexity with scientific novelty.
Do not interpret a high metric as sufficient evidence of methodological rigor.
Prioritize corrections that improve validity, transparency, reproducibility, and alignment with the research questions.

Begin by identifying the thesis chapters and declared research questions. Then construct the traceability matrix before performing the detailed audit.
