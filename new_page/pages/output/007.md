Evaluation: Scientific and Deployment-Grade Validation

Overview
This section delineates a rigorous validation plan for evaluating LLM-enabled ESG/climate disclosure analytics pipelines on machine-readable inputs (digital PDFs, HTML, filings). It emphasizes ground-truth strategies, multifaceted metrics beyond simple accuracy, an error taxonomy by pipeline stage, and reproducibility practices to support credible scientific findings and deployment readiness. The goal is to quantify performance, diagnose failure modes, and provide auditable evidence of reliability across layout parsing, retrieval, LLM extraction, and normalization, while excluding OCR-specific metrics as per scope.

Ground truth strategies

Expert-labeled gold standards
Assemble a panel of domain-expert annotators (ESG/reporting analysts, climate finance researchers) to create gold annotations for a representative sample of documents, including target disclosures, baselines, dates, and provenance-linked evidence (Schmoll, 2025; Beck et al., 2025; .
Use adjudication rounds where disagreements are resolved by senior experts to yield high-quality ground truth for both spans and structured fields Beck et al., 2025; .
Weak/heuristic labels for scale
Generate weak labels via rule-based extractors (e.g., heuristics for standard phrases, table headers, and known GHG scopes) to bootstrap larger datasets, with subsequent human correction to converge toward higher precision (Schmoll, 2025; .
Model-assisted labeling
Employ a cycle where automated extractions are reviewed by humans, who can correct, augment, or annotate uncertainties; feed corrected examples back to retrain or fine-tune encoders or prompts, enabling progressive improvement (Schmoll, 2025; .
Adjudication and inter-stage validation
Implement cross-stage adjudication where discrepancies in ground-truth at the extraction or normalization stage trigger targeted expert review to ensure consistency across stages (layout parsing, chunking, retrieval, extraction) Beck et al., 2025; .
Metrics beyond accuracy

Field-level and record-level metrics
Field-level F1: precision and recall for individual fields (e.g., target, baseline year, unit, scope) within extracted records.
Record-level exact match: binary score indicating whether an entire extracted record matches the ground truth across all fields.
Evidence and provenance metrics
Evidence correctness: proportion of extracted claims that have correct, verifiable supporting passages; measure alignment between fields and the retrieved evidence set.
Provenance fidelity: degree to which each extracted item is linked to the exact source fragment (section/table/footnote) with correct anchors.
Abstention and calibration
Abstention rate: proportion of items for which the system correctly abstains due to insufficient confidence or unresolved ambiguity.
Confidence calibration: reliability diagrams and expected calibration error (ECE) to assess whether model-provided confidences align with observed accuracy.
Robustness and generalization
Cross-domain/generalization scores: performance on out-of-sample jurisdictions, languages (where applicable), and reporting standards.
Temporal drift detection: monitoring drops in metrics over time that may indicate model drift or standard updates.
Efficiency and operational metrics
Latency per document and per extraction; throughput under realistic deployment constraints.
Resource usage: compute footprint, memory, and scaling properties with increasing document volumes.
Explainability and auditability
Traceability coverage: fraction of extractions with complete end-to-end provenance (source passages, page/section anchors, and timestamps).
Human-review load: volume of items flagged for human review relative to total extractions, indicating the effort required for maintaining reliability.
Error taxonomy by stage

1) Layout parsing and layout-aware parsing
Common failures
Misordering of text due to irregular reading order, multi-column layouts, rotated tables, or missing captions.
Incorrect table geometry interpretation, e.g., merged cells, spanning headers, or mis-assigned row/column roles.
Footnotes and appendices mis-linked or dropped, leading to incomplete provenance.
Evaluation hooks
Layout fidelity metrics: alignment of extracted sections/headers with ground truth; table-cell alignment accuracy; caption–content linkage.
Proximity/linkage correctness: accuracy of linking narrative statements to the proper table rows and footnotes.
2) Retrieval and indexing
Common failures
Incomplete or biased retrieval, missing key corroborating passages, or stale context from outdated standards.
Over-retrieval of generic sources at the expense of authoritative references; insufficient multi-hop evidence chaining.
Evaluation hooks
Retrieval precision/recall on ground-truth evidence sets; coverage of required corroborating passages per extraction.
Evidence-augmentation rate: fraction of extractions fortified by retrieved passages; relevance and recency checks.
3) LLM extraction
Common failures
Hallucinations or mislabeling of targets vs. performance; incorrect baselines or horizons without provenance.
Prompt sensitivity causing variability across documents or over time; inconsistent field definitions.
Evaluation hooks
Field-level F1, precision, recall for each extracted attribute; provenance linkage percentage; calibration of confidence scores.
Cross-document consistency checks: agreement of identical extractions across related documents/years under controlled prompts.
4) Normalization and entity resolution
Common failures
Incorrect disambiguation of entities (companies, standards) across jurisdictions; mis-mapped scopes or units.
Loss of nuance in hedged language affecting boundary semantics (e.g., “targets” vs. “plans”).
Evaluation hooks
Entity resolution accuracy against gold-standard canonicalizations; cross-document consistency rates; unit and scope normalization accuracy.
5) Knowledge representation / lineage
Common failures
Provenance drift or incomplete lineage, leading to gaps in traceability; schema drift as standards evolve.
Evaluation hooks
Lineage correctness: end-to-end verification that each fact traces to the cited source; audit trail completeness.
Graph/query validity: correctness and performance of common queries over the knowledge representation; versioning integrity checks.
Reproducibility guidance

Versioning
Use strict version control for datasets, ground-truth annotations, prompts, and model checkpoints; maintain immutable snapshots for each evaluation run.
Record document revisions, standard versions (e.g., IFRS/S1, GRI), and any corrigenda, enabling reproducible cross-study comparisons.
Logging prompts and configurations
Log all prompts, prompts variants, and model parameters; store retrieval context and produced outputs with timestamped references to source passages.
Maintain a prompt registry with metadata (purpose, stage, language, per-document variations) to facilitate ablation studies and audits.
Canary sets and rollouts
Maintain small, representative canary document sets updated periodically to validate pipeline behavior after changes; perform staged rollout with monitoring before full deployment.
Implement A/B testing or controlled experiments to compare alternative prompts, retrieval strategies, or decoding constraints, with pre-registered success criteria.
Reproducible evaluation pipelines
Use deterministic inference settings where possible; document random seeds and sampling strategies; share evaluation scripts and gold standards where permissible.
Provide end-to-end reproducible pipelines (data ingest → parsing → extraction → normalization → knowledge store) with traceable execution logs and provenance.
Compact rubric: evidence-grounded extraction evaluation (3–6 criteria)

Proximity-anchored provenance: Are all extracted fields tied to explicit source passages (section/table/footnote) with correct anchors?
Evidence alignment accuracy: Do the corroborating passages precisely support the extracted value(s) and boundaries?
Field fidelity: Are numeric values, units, scopes, baselines, and time horizons correctly identified and normalized?
Abstention and calibration: Is abstention applied appropriately where evidence is insufficient or ambiguous; are confidence scores well-calibrated?
Cross-document consistency: Are identical facts consistent across related documents/years under identical prompts?
Auditability: Is the entire extraction chain traceable to raw source material and versioned metadata?
Inter-annotator agreement and disagreement handling

Agreement assessment
Have multiple annotators independently labeled a subset of items? Compute conventional agreement metrics (e.g., Cohen’s kappa) to quantify reliability on both span and field-label tasks.
Adjudication workflow
When disagreements arise, an adjudicator reviews evidence, reconciles labels, and records rationale; implement tie-breaking rules and document decision criteria.
Document disagreements and resolutions to inform future guideline refinements and annotation guidelines.
Training and guidelines
Provide annotators with detailed guidelines, exemplar cases (typical targets, baselines, hedges), and decision trees to reduce variability.
Impact on evaluation
Report inter-annotator agreement alongside downstream model performance to contextualize variability; high agreement strengthens confidence in ground truth, while lower agreement signals areas needing clearer guidelines or more robust extraction strategies.
