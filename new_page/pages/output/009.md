Future Work

Open challenges and trajectories to advance robust, interpretable, and scalable ESG/climate disclosure analytics on machine-readable documents.

1) Tables and figures: accurate extraction and interpretation from complex tabular structures

Why hard
Tables frequently feature merged cells, multi-row/column headers, footnotes, and embedded sub-tables, complicating reliable cell-to-field mappings and downstream normalization. Variations across formats (PDF tables, HTML tables, image-based tables in scans) exacerbate alignment and semantic anchoring.
Progress signals
Benchmarks that measure table-cell alignment accuracy, header-root mappings, and footnote linkage to corresponding narrative claims; evaluation of end-to-end extraction of key KPIs from tables (emissions, baselines, years) with provenance. Progress demonstrated by improved table reconstruction fidelity and provenance tagging in layout-aware parsing (Mishra et al., 2024; Chen, 2025)Chen, 2025; .
2) Multilingual alignment and cross-language normalization

Why hard
ESG disclosures span many languages with divergent terminologies, units, and standards; cross-language mapping of concepts (e.g., scopes, baselines, metrics) is nontrivial and error-prone without language-aware normalization rules.
Progress signals
Multilingual benchmarks for cross-lingual extraction and alignment, including calibrated evaluation of boundary detection, unit normalization, and standard-mapping across languages; demonstrated benefits of language-aware prompts and cross-language datasets Ongan et al., 2025; Alam et al., 2021)Ruder et al., 2021; Zhou, 2025).
3) Evidence-grounded extraction at scale

Why hard
Ensuring that every extracted fact is backed by explicit, traceable passages across diverse parts of the document (tables, footnotes, appendices) while maintaining consistent provenance is difficult at scale, especially with hedged language and jurisdictional nuance.
Progress signals
Development of evidence-coverage metrics, provenance-aware evaluation, and end-to-end audits showing high rate of passage-backed extractions with calibrated confidence; use of RAG and structured prompts to anchor outputs to retrieved passages (Khak et al., 2025; Chen, 2025)Zhao et al., 2025; Cui, 2025; Chen et al., 2022).
4) Cross-document consistency and longitudinal alignment

Why hard
Firms publish yearly disclosures with evolving standards; ensuring that entities, scopes, and baselines remain consistently linked across years requires robust entity resolution and temporal lineage, even when wording changes.
Progress signals
Longitudinal benchmarks assessing consistency of extracted records across years for the same entity, with metrics capturing drift in scope definitions and normalization, plus evaluation of provenance continuity over time (Ni et al., 2023; Chen, 2025)Cui, 2025; .
5) Multimodal and layout-robust parsing beyond text

Why hard
Visual cues (tables, charts, figure captions) and layout artifacts influence meaning but are not captured by pure text; robust parsing must integrate multimodal signals to preserve semantic units.
Progress signals
Benchmarks that jointly evaluate textual extraction with tables/figures alignment, caption anchoring, and visual context integration; improved performance of layout-flow models and multimodal parsers on ESG documents Chen, 2025)Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; Chen, 2025).
6) Tables/figures: robust table-to-entity linking

Why hard
Mapping each table cell or chart value to a canonical entity, metric, and jurisdiction requires precise linking, especially when tables are nested or embedded in narrative sections with hedging.
Progress signals
Evaluation of table-to-entity linking accuracy, error analyses by table type, and improvements in linking baselines with target values to canonical KPI fields; demonstration of improved extraction fidelity when table structure is explicitly modeled (Mishra et al., 2024; Chen, 2025).
7) Bias, representativeness, and region/language fairness

Why hard
Data sources, ground-truths, and evaluation sets often overweight certain regions or sectors; performance gaps across languages and markets can lead to skewed deployment outcomes.
Progress signals
Stratified evaluation metrics by region/sector/language, reporting of calibration curves per jurisdiction, and benchmarks that reveal differential performance; ongoing collection of diverse ground-truth datasets Beck et al., 2025)Vardhan et al., 2023; Ongan et al., 2025; Alam et al., 2021).
8) Privacy, licensing, and data retention in deployment

Why hard
While disclosures are public, retrieval datasets may include restricted or proprietary content; governance requires careful handling of licenses, data retention, and access controls, especially for sharing benchmarks or analysis outputs.
Progress signals
Documentation of licenses, provenance metadata, and retention policies; audits of compliance in deployed pipelines and transparent disclosures of data-use constraints.
9) Ground-truth scalability and annotation realism

Why hard
High-quality ground-truth for ESG extraction is costly; scalable annotation requires expert adjudication and careful guidelines to ensure consistency across annotators, languages, and standards.
Progress signals
Increasing gold-standard corpora size with multi-annotator consensus and adjudicated ground-truth, plus reporting of inter-annotator agreement alongside model performance to contextualize results (Schmoll, 2025; Beck et al., 2025).
10) Evaluation under shifting standards and regulatory contexts

Why hard
ESG frameworks and disclosure requirements evolve (e.g., new IFRS standards, EU Taxonomy updates); validating models under changing standards requires flexible evaluation protocols and forward-looking benchmarks.
Progress signals
Benchmark suites that incorporate versioned standards and forward-compatibility tests; regression tests that quantify degradation when standards update; and transparent reporting of model adaptation needs (Schmoll, 2025; Yang et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2020).
Cross-cutting methodological directions

Reproducibility and transparency
Emphasis on versioned ground-truth datasets, prompts, model checkpoints, and evaluation scripts; canary sets and staged rollouts to observe performance before broad deployment.
Benchmarking and community datasets
Development of open, multilingual, and multi-jurisdictional benchmark suites for table extraction, table-to-field linking, and evidence-grounded evaluation to enable fair comparisons across methods.
Interpretability and auditability
Continued investment in provenance, confidence calibration, and explainable outputs to support decision-makers and regulators in interpreting automated extractions.
Note: The challenges and progress signals above are framed to emphasize practical evaluation and deployment-readiness for ESG/climate disclosure analytics on machine-readable documents. They reflect a blend of layout-aware parsing, multilingual alignment, evidence grounding, and auditability considerations discussed throughout the paper.
