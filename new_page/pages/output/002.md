Abstract: This review synthesizes the state of LLM-assisted ESG and climate-disclosure analytics pipelines with a digital-text focus, clarifying why disclosures remain challenging data even when machine-readable and outlining a pragmatic pipeline architecture. We frame the problem as a data-quality and structure issue: climate- and ESG-disclosures are often multi-source, narrative, and formatted in heterogeneous templates, yielding semi-structured or unstructured text whose semantic and temporal contexts complicate reliable extraction and comparability Sintas, 2020)Leal, 2023)Kirby, 2023; . To address this, we advocate a layout-aware parsing and chunking paradigm that preserves document structure (sectioning, tables, footnotes) and encodes provenance, enabling consistent segmentation for downstream processing Leal, 2023)Sintas, 2020)(Khak et al., 2025; . Building on this, we describe core pipeline components: (i) layout-aware parsing to detect figures, tables, and sections; (ii) intelligent chunking that respects semantic units (risk disclosures, metrics, targets) and aligns with reporting standards; (iii) retrieval over internal/external ESG and climate document sets to supply context and evidence; (iv) LLM-based extraction and normalization of entities, metrics, and temporality while maintaining traceability to source passages; and (v) evaluation modules that combine automated checks (consistency, calibration of risk metrics, cross-reference validation) with human-in-the-loop reviews to gauge reliability Kaoukis, 2025; (Khak et al., 2025; Kirby, 2023; Sintas, 2020). The main contribution of this work is a taxonomy of LLM-assisted ESG/climate-disclosure pipelines tailored to digital-text workflows, accompanied by practical recommendations for data architecture, tool choices, evaluation protocols, and governance controls that practitioners can implement in near-term deployments Kaoukis, 2025; (Khak et al., 2025; Kirby, 2023; Leal, 2023). We emphasize caution regarding reliability and evaluation: LLM outputs can exhibit hallucinations, misalignment with disclosure standards, and variability across jurisdictions; thus robust evaluation—covering extraction accuracy, lineage tracing, provenance, and out-of-distribution robustness—is essential, with explicit reporting of confidence, limitations, and failure modes to prevent misinterpretation of risk signals (Khak et al., 2025; Kaoukis, 2025; Sintas, 2020). Keywords: ESG disclosures, climate reporting, large language models, layout-aware parsing, retrieval augmented generation, pipeline taxonomy, evaluation, reliability, governance.
