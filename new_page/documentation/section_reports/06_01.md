# 6.1 Contribution Summary

## Section Purpose

This conclusion section synthesizes the research contributions, practical implications, and future work arising from the ESG disclosure tone analysis pipeline. The conclusion is grounded in literature on ESG analytics, sustainability disclosure, climate NLP, machine learning for ESG, and greenwashing detection [R003], [R006], [R007], [R009], [R018].

## Contribution Summary

The research contributes an end-to-end workflow that converts sustainability reports into structured ESG records. It introduces a tone-aware disclosure taxonomy, compares extracted tone with ClimateBERT-style labels, provides visual dashboards, preserves reproducible artifacts, and defines a path toward formal ground-truth benchmarking.

The empirical evidence supports feasibility. The pipeline produced 332 extracted ESG records from 110 successful ESG extraction runs. The dominant tone was `commitment` with 115 records. The strongest validation pattern was the 91 co-occurrences between `commitment` and `climate-commitment`. These numbers show that automated tone-aware ESG analysis can surface meaningful disclosure patterns.

## Answers to Research Questions

RQ1 is answered by the OCR and extraction workflow: PDF-derived sustainability reports can be transformed into markdown and then into structured ESG records.

RQ2 is answered by the extracted fields: records include aspect, ESG pillar, sentiment, tone, labels, and reasoning.

RQ3 is answered by the tone/ClimateBERT comparison: commitment labels align strongly with climate-commitment labels, while missing tones reveal review candidates.

RQ4 is answered by the diagnostic findings: 61 missing tone records, schema drift in sentiment fields, and social underrepresentation expose weaknesses in the current pipeline.

RQ5 is answered by the documentation and dashboard outputs: static charts, CSV tables, markdown reports, and the Streamlit app make the work inspectable and reproducible.

## Practical Implications

For ESG analysts, the pipeline accelerates evidence extraction and helps locate commitment/outcome imbalances. For sustainability report authors, it provides feedback on whether disclosures read as promises, actions, or results. For regulators, especially in contexts such as OJK-aligned sustainability reporting, automated tone classification could support preliminary review and risk screening. For investors, tone distributions could supplement traditional ESG ratings by identifying whether ESG narratives are backed by reported outcomes.

## Future Work

The next stage should build an expert-labeled ground truth dataset. ClimateBERT should be run over all 332 extracted record texts to create one-to-one comparison rows. OCR quality should be measured using character error rate and table extraction accuracy. The greenwashing index should be validated against external ratings or known cases. The ESG ontology should be expanded for Indonesian disclosure topics. Finally, multilingual transformer fine-tuning should be tested using Indonesian and English ESG text.

## Closing Statement

The combined findings demonstrate that automated tone-aware ESG disclosure analysis is feasible, but formal validity depends on stronger ground truth. The current pipeline is therefore best understood as a complete research prototype and benchmark-construction platform.

## Section-Specific Report

The research contributes six concrete outputs: an end-to-end document-to-record pipeline, a tone-aware ESG taxonomy, ClimateBERT-style comparison, interactive visualization, reproducible artifacts, and a benchmark design for future annotation.

RQ1 is answered by the OCR-to-record workflow. RQ2 is answered by the 332 structured ESG records. RQ3 is answered by the 91 co-occurrences between `commitment` and `climate-commitment`. RQ4 is answered by diagnostic weaknesses such as 61 missing tones. RQ5 is answered through static charts, CSV files, markdown documentation, and the Streamlit dashboard. Together, these findings demonstrate the feasibility of automated tone-aware ESG disclosure analysis.
