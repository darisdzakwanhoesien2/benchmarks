## Why this research matters

Every year, hundreds of Indonesian publicly-listed companies publish ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) reports — documents meant to prove that they operate responsibly. But a central, unresolved problem is that nobody has a reliable, systematic way to *read* these reports at scale. They are long (62,000+ tokens on average), bilingual (Indonesian and English mixed within the same document), OCR-extracted from PDFs with varying quality, and span multiple years and sectors. That makes them nearly impossible to compare directly.

The stakes are real. Investors use ESG scores to allocate capital. Regulators use them to assess compliance. Journalists and NGOs use them to hold companies accountable. If the underlying text is processed inconsistently — or worse, if ESG claims sound positive but are never backed by measurable numbers — then greenwashing can pass undetected at an institutional scale.

This research matters because it builds the *infrastructure layer* that all downstream ESG analysis depends on. Without knowing which years are sparsely represented, which pillars dominate the narrative, and whether documents meet a minimum quality threshold, any topic model or sentiment score you build on top will silently inherit those biases. That is the gap no prior Indonesian ESG NLP study has formally closed.
