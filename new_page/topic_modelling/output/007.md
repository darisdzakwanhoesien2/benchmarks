Section 6. Topic Modelling in ESG Disclosure Analysis: What Works in Practice

This section translates the methodological landscape into practical guidance for analyzing ESG disclosures, with a focus on OCR-derived bilingual Indonesian corpora. It covers typical research uses, common failure modes, and best practices tailored to implementation realities.

6.1 Typical research uses

Comparisons across firms or time
Core idea: Use topic distributions to compare thematic emphasis across companies and over years, highlighting shifts in ESG focus and materiality.
Expansion: Researchers often compare pillar-aligned topic prevalence (Environmental, Social, Governance) or cross-sectional topic profiles to identify leadership or laggards in ESG disclosure practices (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019), Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).

Concentration and fragmentation analysis
Core idea: Assess how concentrated topics are within documents or corpora to detect dominant ESG themes and potential boilerplate dominance.
Expansion: Concentration metrics reveal whether a few topics dominate the discourse or if a broad mix of themes exists, informing governance risk signals and materiality mapping (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019).

Evolution and drift over time
Core idea: Track topic trajectories to observe how ESG narratives evolve, potentially in response to regulation, stakeholder pressure, or corporate strategy.
Expansion: Dynamic or hierarchical topic models facilitate temporal trend analysis, enabling horizon-scanning for sustainability developments and governance disclosures across years (Moreno & Caminero, 2020; , Aryoseto et al., 2023).

Auditing and triage for compliance
Core idea: Use topics to flag areas requiring human review or external verification (e.g., environmental liabilities, supply-chain governance).
Expansion: Topic signals support triage workflows for auditors, regulators, and internal compliance teams by prioritizing segments with material risk signals or inconsistent ABSA cues (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019).

ABSA-aligned interpretability and taxonomy mapping
Core idea: Align topics with ESG taxonomies and aspect-based sentiment cues to yield governance-relevant insights.
Expansion: Topic distributions can be linked to ABSA facets (e.g., emissions, labor practices, governance controls) to produce interpretable dashboards for stakeholders and decision makers (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019).

6.2 Common failure modes

Boilerplate dominance
Bullet: Boilerplate and boilerplate-like phrases overwhelm topics, pushing discourse toward generic governance language rather than substantive ESG content.
OCR-induced fragmentation
Bullet: Character-level errors and line-break artifacts fragment tokens, creating spurious topics and reducing coherence.
Code-switching and language-shift misalignment
Bullet: Indonsian-English mixing can yield language-topic separation rather than thematic topics if embeddings or vocabularies are not adequately cross-lingual.
Overfitting to document structure
Bullet: Section headings and template language drive topics that reflect report structure more than material ESG issues.
Short-text sparsity
Bullet: Statement-level units yield very short contexts, resulting in unstable topic assignments and noisy ABSA signals without sufficient aggregation.
Uneven cross-corpus embedding coverage
Bullet: Imbalanced bilingual representations lead to topics that favor one language, undermining cross-language interpretability.
Evaluation bias toward coherence
Bullet: Reliance on intrinsic coherence metrics alone can misrepresent practical usefulness for governance and ABSA alignment.
Insufficient external validation
Bullet: Without mapping to ESG taxonomies and ABSA cues, topics may lack materiality relevance, hindering decision-usefulness.
6.2.1 Linking failure modes to earlier sections

The discussion in Section 2 highlighted why boilerplate, OCR artifacts, and structure can yield junk topics; Section 3’s method families reveal that embedding-based and neural hybrids can mitigate some noise but introduce complexity; Section 4 emphasized boilerplate handling and multilingual preprocessing as a foundation. The failure modes above emerge at the intersection: OCR noise and boilerplate distort topic quality, while evaluation strategies that ignore extrinsic ABSA alignment risk delivering superficially coherent but practically useless topics. The practical takeaway is that robust practice requires combining noise-aware preprocessing (Section 4), multi-scale unit analyses (Section 2), and extrinsic evaluation (Section 5) to avoid the highlighted pitfalls.
6.3 Recommended best practices (for Indonesian ESG OCR corpora)

6.3.1 Best practice checklist (6–10 items)

1) Preprocessing-based baselining (reference: Section 4.1)

Checkpoint: Establish a clean baseline by applying OCR post-correction and layout-aware segmentation before modelling; document the preprocessing version with a checksum.
2) Boilerplate identification and deduplication (reference: Section 4.2)

Checkpoint: Implement deduplication and chunk-based downweighting of boilerplate to reduce non-informative topic signals; report the proportion of boilerplate removed.
3) Language tagging and bilingual representation (reference: Section 4.3)

Checkpoint: Tag segments with language labels and employ multilingual embeddings; verify cross-language topic coherence to avoid language-topic fragmentation.
4) Unit-of-analysis strategy (reference: Section 2.3)

Checkpoint: Define and compare at least two units (statement-level and OCR-chunk) within the same corpus to capture both fine-grained and robust ESG signals; document how boundary definitions affect results.
5) Baseline model deployment (reference: Section 3.1)

Checkpoint: Run LDA/biLDA as a language-agnostic baseline; report coherence and stability metrics to establish a floor for more complex models.
6) Embedding-based methods with cross-lingual alignment (reference: Section 3.3)

Checkpoint: Use embedding-based topic modelling (e.g., BERTopic/ETM) with cross-lingual alignment; assess topic semantic coherence across Indonesian-English content.
7) Extrinsic evaluation plan (reference: Section 5.3)

Checkpoint: Predefine ESG pillars and ABSA aspects; compute pillar distributions and ABSA-aligned scores per document; report entropy or concentration metrics to quantify alignment strength.
8) Stability and reproducibility protocol (reference: Section 5.2)

Checkpoint: Run multi-seed experiments (e.g., 5–10 seeds) and document topic stability (NMI, top-k overlap); publish a reproducibility manifest and corpus signature for each run.
9) Transparent artifact export (reference: Section 4.4)

Checkpoint: Export topic models, labels, ABSA mappings, and metadata in a versioned, export-friendly format (JSON/CSV) with provenance metadata; attach a reproducibility bundle to each experiment.
10) Human interpretability workflow (reference: Section 5.4)

Checkpoint: Implement a structured human labeling protocol with exemplar passages, inter-annotator agreement reporting, and adjudication procedures; reserve space for labeling uncertainty in dashboards.
6.3.2 Practical export and reproducibility considerations

Artifact exports: Save topic descriptors (top keywords, exemplar passages), ABSA-aligned labels, and pillar mappings; include language tags and section-level provenance for traceability.
Versioned corpora: Use data-versioning (DVC or Git-LFS) for OCR corpora, preprocessing configurations, and evaluation results; maintain a manifest with document IDs, checksums, and OCRqualitytags.
Reproducibility: Provide a reproducibility package with code, environment specifications, preprocessing scripts, model configurations, and a fixed random-seed regime; include a short README describing the data provenance and typical runtime.
End of Section 6.

Note: The best-practice items reference the preprocessing (Section 4), unit-of-analysis decisions (Section 2.3), intrinsic and extrinsic evaluation (Sections 5.1–5.3), and artifact export/reproducibility considerations (Section 4.4). The recommendations are designed to be actionable for Indonesian ESG OCR corpora and to align with the review’s implementation-first, evaluation-first stance.
