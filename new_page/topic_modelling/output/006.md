Section 5. Evaluation: Beyond Coherence

This section outlines a multi-faceted evaluation framework for topic modelling on OCR-derived, bilingual Indonesian ESG disclosures. It emphasizes moving beyond intrinsic coherence to assess stability, robustness, extrinsic alignment with ESG taxonomies and ABSA signals, and human interpretability. Each subsection provides concrete procedures, metrics, and caveats tailored to long, noisy, code-switched ESG corpora.

5.1 Intrinsic evaluation

Core ideas:

Intrinsic metrics probe the internal quality of topics (coherence, diversity, and overlap) without requiring external labels.
In ESG OCR corpora, these metrics can be misleading if they reflect boilerplate signal, OCR artifacts, or language-only clustering rather than meaningful sustainability themes.
Key points:

Coherence measures (e.g., C_V, UCI, NPMI) may be inflated by boilerplate or section headings that co-occur across many documents; such signals do not guarantee interpretability of material ESG content (Strien et al., 2020; , Bowen & Min, 2023; , Zhou et al., 2023; .
Diversity and topic overlap can be artificially high when topics share boilerplate vocabularies or when multilingual tokens split thematic sentences; OCR noise can artificially fragment topics, creating brittle or redundant clusters Zosa et al., 2021; , Zhou et al., 2023; , Park et al., 2022).
For bilingual ESG corpora, coherence can be biased toward one language if embeddings or vocabularies are imbalanced; cross-language topics may appear less coherent if cross-lingual alignment is weak, even when substantive themes exist (Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).
Recommendation: report a multi-metric intrinsic suite (e.g., coherence, distinctiveness, and approximate topic overlap) and annotate which topics are boilerplate-driven versus content-driven. Complement intrinsic metrics with extrinsic validation to avoid over-reliance on coherence alone (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Zhou et al., 2023; .
5.2 Stability and robustness

Core idea:

A reproducible stability protocol quantifies how resistant topics are to random initialization, data perturbations, and OCR noise.
Protocol (reproducible stability):

Seeds: Run each model with a fixed set of random seeds (e.g., 5–10 seeds) and report mean and standard deviation of coherence and topic label stability.
Resampling: Create multiple bootstrap samples of documents (e.g., 80/20 train-test-like splits or year-wise splits) and compute topic-wise Jaccard similarity or Hungarian-aligned topic mappings across folds.
Topic overlap measures: Use metrics such as normalized mutual information (NMI) between topic assignments across runs, and topic stability scores that quantify how much the top-k words per topic persist across runs.
Reporting format: Present per-model stability summaries (mean ± SD) for coherence, NMI, and top-word overlap, plus a cross-run heatmap of topic correspondences. Include a narrative on which topics are stable and which drift, with identification of boilerplate-dominated topics as a potential instability source.
Practical notes: For OCR-bearing data, include an OCR-perturbed replicate (e.g., simulate additional noise or re-run with a corrected subset) to estimate robustness to residual OCR errors (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Zosa et al., 2021; , Park et al., 2022).
5.3 Extrinsic evaluation using supervised labels (ABSA alignment)

Core idea:

Extrinsic evaluation grounds topics in domain-relevant signals by aligning them with ESG pillars and ABSA aspects, enabling interpretable, decision-relevant insights.
Procedure:

Pillar/aspect distributions: Map each topic to ESG pillars (Environmental, Social, Governance) and to ABSA aspects (e.g., emissions, supply chain, governance controls). For each document, compute the topic distribution and derive the per-pillars distribution by summing topic weights associated with each pillar.
Interpretation: High weight on an environmental topic within a report segment that discusses emissions or policy indicates meaningful alignment; misalignment (environmental terms in non-environmental contexts) flags noise or mislabelling.
Quantitative alignment metric idea: Entropy-based alignment score.
Compute the distribution of topic weights across pillars for each document, then compute the entropy H(P) of that distribution. Lower entropy indicates stronger pillar focus (good alignment), while higher entropy indicates diffuse topic assignment across pillars.
Alternatively compute concentration index (e.g., Gini or Simpson index) over pillar weights to assess whether topics concentrate on a few ESG axes.
Caveats:
The mapping from topics to pillars depends on labeling quality; provide a transparent labeling schema and multiple annotators to establish a reliable mapping.
Intrinsic NLP signals may not perfectly reflect materiality; cross-check with external ESG taxonomy references and ABSA cues to avoid overfitting to the training corpus.
In bilingual settings, ensure that cross-language topics contribute to a unified pillar distribution rather than splitting signals by language.
Practical outcome: Report pillar/ABSA alignment as a set of per-document distributions plus aggregate statistics (means, medians) across corpora; present qualitative examples illustrating correct and incorrect alignments.
5.4 Human interpretability protocol

Step-by-step labeling protocol:
1) Topic presentation: For each topic, present the top-10 words (and top-5 exemplars from representative documents) and a short candidate label.
2) Dual-label labeling: Have two or more annotators assign:

A human-readable topic label (based on the exemplar documents and keywords).
A mapping to ESG pillars (E, S, G) and ABSA aspects (e.g., emissions, water, labor practices, governance controls).
3) Exemplar review: Annotators select 2–3 exemplar passages (quotes) per topic that best illustrate the theme, including both Indonesian and English passages when present.
4) Confidence scoring: Annotators rate confidence (e.g., 1–5) for each topic label and pillar/ABSA mapping.
5) Reconciliation: Compute inter-annotator agreement (e.g., Cohen’s kappa for discrete labels, Krippendorff’s alpha for ordinal confidence); resolve disagreements via adjudication discussion to produce a final consensus labeling.
Inter-annotator disagreement:

What it implies: Moderate disagreement often reflects genuine ambiguity in multilingual ESG discourse, overlapping topics, or subtleties in interpretation. High disagreement signals topics that are ill-defined or overly boilerplate-driven.
How to handle: Report disagreement statistics alongside final labels; provide notes on topics with persistent disagreements and consider re-segmentation or re-annotation with refined guidelines.
Impact on evaluation: Disagreements highlight areas where topic modelling may require hierarchical or multi-label representations and where external taxonomy anchors (ABSA cues, ESG standards) are especially valuable.
End of Section 5.

Notes:

The evaluation framework emphasizes extrinsic validity and human interpretability in addition to intrinsic metrics, acknowledging the unique challenges posed by OCR noise and bilingual ESG corpora.
Where possible, anchor evaluations with external ESG taxonomies (e.g., GRI-based mappings) and ABSA signals to ensure topics translate into governance-relevant, decision-ready insights.
The proposed protocols are designed to be incrementally adoptable in an implementation-focused review, enabling practitioners to compare baselines and advanced methods on a common, transparent evaluation plane.
For OCR robustness and topic evaluation fundamentals, see works on OCR impact on NLP tasks and embedding-based topic models under noisy conditions (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Zosa et al., 2021; , Zhou et al., 2023; , Park et al., 2022).
For cross-language topic modelling and ABSA-aligned evaluation paradigms, see multilingual topic modeling literature and ABSA integration experiments in ESG-like domains (Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).
General LDA foundational material and topic coherence discussions are drawn from foundational surveys and applied studies cited in Section 3.
