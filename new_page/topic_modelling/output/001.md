Abstract

Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) reporting increasingly hinges on structured textual analysis of long, OCR-derived documents. In Indonesian corporate contexts, ESG sustainability reports are frequently produced in bilingual (Indonesian-English) formats and are digitized via OCR, which introduces layered noise (character-level errors, segmentation issues, and layout-induced distortions) that degrade downstream NLP signals. This review addresses the core problem: how OCR noise, combined with long documents and bilingual mixing, challenges topic modelling for ESG content and why robust modelling matters for ESG decision-making, compliance, and stakeholder transparency. We synthesize evidence across OCR-robustness studies, topic modelling in noisy text, and multiscript/bilingual NLP to articulate the risk that OCR errors and language mixing pose to topic coherence, topic stability, and downstream ESG insights, while highlighting their implications for governance and sustainability analyses (Strien et al., 2020; , Zosa et al., 2021; , Alexander & Pujawan, 2024; , Hamdi et al., 2022). Where the literature shows mixed findings—e.g., embedding-based models offering modest resilience to OCR noise relative to classical LDA, and context-dependent degradation of topic quality with OCR errors—this review clarifies the conditions under which topic modelling remains informative for ESG discourse extraction in Indonesian contexts Zosa et al., 2021; , (Strien et al., 2020; , Alexander & Pujawan, 2024; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; .

Contributions of this review include: (i) a taxonomy of method families currently used for OCR-derived ESG text, including traditional LDA, neural and embedding-enhanced topic models, and hybrid approaches that leverage contextual embeddings for Indonesian-English corpora Zosa et al., 2021; , (Strien et al., 2020; , Alexander & Pujawan, 2024; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; ; (ii) a consolidated preprocessing pipeline tailored to bilingual OCR ESG reports, detailing noise-aware tokenization, script/orthography handling, and OCR post-correction considerations that influence downstream topic discovery (Strien et al., 2020; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; ; (iii) evaluation frameworks for OCR-impacted topic models, emphasizing coherence, stability, and cross-ontology alignment with ESG taxonomies and ABSA (Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis) signals, with cross-reference to best practices in extrinsic OCR evaluation for NLP tasks (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022), Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; ; (iv) integration pathways for coupling topic models with ABSA and ESG taxonomy constructs to yield interpretable topics aligned to sustainability dimensions (environmental impact, governance disclosures, social programs) and facilitate traceable decision-support outcomes (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022), Moreno & Caminero, 2020); and (v) an implementation roadmap that operationalizes a structured synthesis into a practical pipeline for researchers and practitioners: data collection of Indonesian-English ESG reports, controlled OCR quality assessment, multi-model topic experiments, ABSA-tuned topic interpretation, taxonomy mapping, and an iterative, deployment-ready workflow with reproducibility-focused guidelines (datasets, code, evaluation metrics) Zosa et al., 2021; , (Strien et al., 2020; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .

We conclude with a structured synthesis that emphasizes five pragmatic dimensions: (1) preprocessing and OCR noise management as a critical enabler of usable topics; (2) method selection guidance tailored to bilingual ESG corpora and document length; (3) evaluation practices that couple topic quality with ESG-relevant interpretability and ABSA cues; (4) alignment with ESG taxonomy and ABSA to ensure actionable topics; and (5) a concrete, implementation-oriented roadmap that researchers can adapt to real-world Indonesian ESG reporting workflows. Across these dimensions, the review articulates nuanced positions where OCR noise can either obscure or, with appropriate modelling choices, preserve the integrity of ESG topic signals in bilingual, OCR-derived Indonesian reports (Strien et al., 2020; , Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Hamdi et al., 2022).
