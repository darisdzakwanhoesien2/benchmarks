Introduction
The ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) paradigm has become central to corporate accountability, investment decision-making, and stakeholder communication. ESG sustainability reports are often expansive, spanning hundreds to thousands of pages, with standardized disclosures across jurisdictions and industries. Reading such volumes in full is infeasible for researchers, regulators, and practitioners who require timely insights for governance, risk assessment, and policy enforcements. In Indonesian contexts, ESG disclosures frequently accompany multilingual content and long-form narratives, amplifying the information-processing burden. This review centers on this practical information-access gap and motivates the development of scalable, automated extraction of ESG themes from large OCR-derived corpora, enabling reproducible synthesis and actionable insights (Strien et al., 2020; , (Zosa et al., 2021; , Hamdi et al., 2022; .

Topic modelling offers an attractive solution to this problem by aggregating themes into interpretable topics without requiring manual labeling. However, its use in ESG contexts is often misapplied or overstated. Traditional topic models (e.g., LDA) can deliver coarse or unstable topics when faced with noisy OCR text, multilingual code-switching, and highly structured, boilerplate-rich reports common in ESG disclosures Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; . Embedding-based and transformer-informed topic models (e.g., BERTopic, Top2Vec) have shown promise in producing coherent topics in noisy and multilingual settings, yet their performance is sensitive to OCR quality, language mixing, and document length. The literature indicates that OCR errors degrade topic coherence and stability, sometimes disproportionately across languages, which in turn undermines interpretability and vosibility for ESG decision-support (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022). The consequence for ESG is nontrivial: misidentified topics can obscure material sustainability issues, hinder comparability across companies, and impair oversight and stakeholder communication. Therefore, a careful, evidence-backed, and implementation-oriented treatment of OCR-affected, bilingual ESG corpora is required to ensure reliable topic-based ESG insights (Strien et al., 2020; , (Zosa et al., 2021; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; .

This review adopts a unique focus at the intersection of Indonesian ESG disclosures, bilingual OCR-derived text, and an implementation-first, evaluation-first research design. The Indonesian ESG reporting landscape presents a combination of language mixing (Indonesian-English code-switching), OCR-derived text with document-level noise, and long-form, heterogeneous reporting structures. By concentrating on this setting, the review synthesizes evidence on method families, preprocessing strategies, and evaluation paradigms that are specifically attuned to the challenges of bilingual, long, OCR-affected ESG corpora. We furthermore emphasize integration with ABSA (Aspect-Based Sentiment Analysis) and ESG taxonomies to produce interpretable, governance-relevant topics, and we offer an explicit, implementation-oriented roadmap to guide researchers and practitioners from data collection to deployment-ready workflows. Where relevant, we discuss nuances and disagreements across sources regarding model robustness, evaluation metrics, and practical trade-offs (e.g., coherence vs. stability, intrinsic vs. extrinsic evaluation) to present a balanced perspective grounded in evidence (Strien et al., 2020; , (Zosa et al., 2021; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).

1.1 Scope and objectives of this review

This review is guided by four clearly defined objectives:

Catalog and categorize the method families used for OCR-derived ESG topic modelling, with attention to Indonesian-English bilingual corpora and long-form documents, including traditional LDA, neural/embedding-based topic models (e.g., BERTopic, ETM), and hybrid approaches that leverage contextual representations (Strien et al., 2020; , (Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Propose a noise-aware preprocessing and OCR post-correction workflow tailored to bilingual ESG reports, detailing tokenization, script handling, and boilerplate/layout mitigation that materially affect topic discovery and interpretability (Strien et al., 2020; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).
Outline evaluation frameworks that jointly assess topic quality (coherence, stability) and ESG interpretability, including alignment with ESG taxonomies and signals from ABSA, while incorporating extrinsic OCR evaluation considerations relevant to large, bilingual corpora (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).
Describe integration pathways from topic models to ABSA and ESG taxonomy constructs to yield actionable, traceable insights, and provide an implementation roadmap that practitioners can operationalize (data collection, OCR quality control, multi-model experimentation, taxonomy mapping, reproducibility practices) in Indonesian ESG workflows (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).
1.2 Target corpus characteristics (Indonesian ESG disclosures)

OCR noise: ESG reports digitized via OCR exhibit character-level and segmentation errors, which degrade tokenization, topic token co-occurrence patterns, and subsequent topic coherence. These effects propagate into topic stability and interpretability, posing practical barriers to reliable ESG content extraction from large document sets (Strien et al., 2020; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Bilingual mix: Indonesian ESG disclosures commonly mix Indonesian and English segments, or present code-switching phenomena. Multilingual embeddings and cross-language topic modelling can help, but performance is contingent on language mix, domain vocabulary, and the quality of cross-lingual representations; literature indicates that multilingual contexts can both enable broader coverage and introduce additional noise challenges that must be managed carefully (Strien et al., 2020; , (Zosa et al., 2021; , (Xu et al., 2025; , Kumari et al., 2025; , Pikuliak et al., 2023).
Long documents: ESG reports often comprise lengthy narratives, appendices, and boilerplate sections. Long-form text interacts with topic modelling in terms of topic drift, computational efficiency, and the need for hierarchical or dynamic topic representations to capture evolving sustainability themes over sections or years (Strien et al., 2020; , Moreno & Caminero, 2020; , Aryoseto et al., 2023).
Boilerplate and heterogeneous structure: ESG reports frequently contain boilerplate language, governance templates, and heterogeneous sections (environmental disclosures, sustainability metrics, governance statements). Such boilerplate can inflate topic redundancy and obscure material topics unless preprocessing includes boilerplate identification and structural segmentation, enabling more meaningful topic discovery (Fantini, 2016; , Birim, 2024; , Supiadin & Laksito, 2023).
Why these properties matter: OCR noise, bilingual mixing, and document length jointly influence the reliability and utility of topics for ESG insights, including the detection of material issues, cross-company comparability, and governance- and ABSA-aligned interpretability. Acknowledging these corpus characteristics is essential for designing robust pipelines and credible evaluation protocols in Indonesian ESG contexts (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).
Notes on evidence and nuance

Across sources, embedding-based topic models (e.g., BERTopic, ETM) tend to offer improved resilience to OCR noise relative to classical LDA, but gains are conditional on OCR quality and multilingual representations; literature reports both benefits and caveats, highlighting the need for careful preprocessing and evaluation (Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Evaluation literature shows that intrinsic coherence, stability, and extrinsic alignment with domain taxonomies (and ABSA signals) are all valuable; some studies emphasize that OCR-affected data can necessitate extrinsic evaluation to validate practical usefulness in ESG contexts (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022).
The Indonesian-English bilingual setting presents both opportunities for richer semantic capture and challenges for cross-lingual topic alignment, necessitating models that support multilingual embeddings and cross-language topic representations with careful validation (Xu et al., 2025; , Kumari et al., 2025; , Pikuliak et al., 2023).
