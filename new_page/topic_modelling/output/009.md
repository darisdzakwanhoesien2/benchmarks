1) Standardized benchmarks for OCR-impaired bilingual ESG corpora

Gap: There is no widely adopted, open benchmark suite of Indonesian-English ESG reports with controlled OCR noise levels and bilingual segments for topic modelling evaluation.
Why it matters: Without standardized benchmarks, comparing topic models (BoW, embedding-based, neural hybrids) across OCR conditions and bilingual content is inconsistent, hindering progress in repository-ready pipelines for Indonesian ESG analysis.
Concrete next study/experiment: Construct a benchmark corpus of Indonesian ESG reports (with aligned Indonesian-English sections) at multiple OCR quality levels (synthetic noise added to clean transcripts and real OCR outputs). Provide ground-truth topics and ABSA-aligned labels for evaluation. Run a community challenge comparing LDA, NMF, BERTopic/ETM, and neural-topic models, reporting intrinsic and extrinsic metrics, with a public leaderboard and dataset release.
2) Cross-language topic interpretability under code-switching

Gap: Code-switching and mixed-language content in Indonesian ESG reports produce topics that either split by language or conflate linguistic signals with thematic signals.
Why it matters: In Indonesian ESG reporting, bilingual segments can obscure material environmental, social, and governance themes if topics are language-pure rather than semantically aligned across languages.
Concrete next study/experiment: Develop and test cross-lingual topic modelling with explicit cross-language alignment priors (e.g., shared topic embeddings with language-conditioned adapters). Evaluate using ABSA-aligned annotation across Indonesian and English passages, reporting cross-language topic coherence and ABSA-consistent labeling rates. Compare against language-tagged baselines to quantify gains from true cross-language alignment.
3) Robustness to OCR-specific error patterns beyond baseline noise

Gap: Many studies simulate OCR noise but do not systematically evaluate real-world OCR error typologies (e.g., layout-induced merges/splits, column/page segmentation errors, symbol misrecognitions) on long ESG reports.
Why it matters: Indonesian ESG reports are long, boilerplate-rich documents scanned with varied quality; OCR artifacts can disproportionately distort token sequences and sentence boundaries, degrading topic quality.
Concrete next study/experiment: Create a taxonomy of OCR error types observed in Indonesian ESG reports (manual annotation of a representative sample). Design an error-aware preprocessing and training objective that downweights or corrects error-prone patterns during embedding or topic inference. Compare baseline topics with and without error-aware processing under realistic OCR error injections calibrated to the observed taxonomy.
4) Integration with ESG taxonomy and ABSA in low-resource settings

Gap: ABSA-aligned evaluation and ESG taxonomy mapping are often demonstrated in well-resourced domains; Indonesian ESG contexts may lack high-quality, domain-specific ABSA resources.
Why it matters: Practical ESG insights require topics that align with material aspects and sentiment cues; without robust ABSA alignment, topics risk being descriptively coherent but not decision-useful.
Concrete next study/experiment: Develop a lightweight ABSA annotation protocol for Indonesian-English ESG passages (environmental metrics, governance controls, social programs) and a small annotated corpus to bootstrap cross-lingual ABSA. Evaluate topic-to-ABSA alignment using entropy/concentration metrics, and compare multi-task learning models that jointly optimize topic discovery and ABSA labeling versus sequential pipelines.
5) Multi-scale topic modelling with hierarchical and dynamic extensions for ESG evolution

Gap: ESG narratives evolve over time and across sections; flat topic models struggle to capture longitudinal shifts and hierarchical governance structures.
Why it matters: Indonesian ESG disclosures incorporate evolving regulatory expectations and sector-specific sustainability issues; capturing temporal dynamics and hierarchy enhances interpretability and auditability.
Concrete next study/experiment: Implement a hierarchical dynamic topic model that links section-level topics to document-level topics and tracks topic trajectories across years and sectors. Evaluate against a bilingual corpus, focusing on consistency of topic evolution with known policy milestones and sector-specific drivers. Compare to non-hierarchical baselines to quantify gains in interpretability and temporal stability.
6) Boilerplate decontamination at scale with automated validation

Gap: Boilerplate remains a dominant confounder; fully automatic, scalable, and auditable decontamination remains underdeveloped.
Why it matters: In Indonesian ESG reports, boilerplate sections (governance statements, disclaimers) are highly repetitive and can overwhelm true ESG signals, biasing intrinsic evaluations and downstream ABSA mappings.
Concrete next study/experiment: Build an unsupervised boilerplate detector using structural cues, boilerplate fingerprinting, and cross-document repetition metrics; implement automatic deduplication and downweighting in topic modelling. Validate by measuring the reduction in boilerplate signal in top-topic word lists and improvements in ABSA-aligned interpretations across multiple corporations and years.
7) Reproducibility-friendly, repository-ready pipelines for Indonesia

Gap: End-to-end pipelines from OCR-cleaning to ABSA-aligned topics require rigorous reproducibility but are not universally packaged for Indonesian ESG work.
Why it matters: A reproducible, shareable pipeline accelerates adoption by researchers and practitioners in Indonesia, enabling government, regulators, and industry to rely on robust topic-based ESG insights.
Concrete next study/experiment: Develop a turnkey, containerized pipeline with modular components for OCR cleaning, bilingual tokenization, topic modelling (BoW, embedding-based, neural hybrids), ABSA alignment, and ESG taxonomy mapping. Release a minimal reproducibility package (manifest, environment, seeds, and example datasets) with an accompanying tutorial and evaluation suite, and encourage adoption through a GitHub repository with contribution guidelines.
