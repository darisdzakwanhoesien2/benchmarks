Section 3. Families of Topic Modelling Methods

This section surveys core families of topic modelling methods relevant to OCR-derived, bilingual ESG disclosures, with concise statements of how each approach operates, and a balanced view of strengths and weaknesses in the Indonesian-English ESG setting. Each subsection concludes with guidance on where the method fits in an evaluation pipeline, starting from baselines to more advanced hybrids.

3.1 Probabilistic topic models (LDA-style)

Core idea:
Probabilistic topic models treat each document as a mixture of latent topics, where each topic is a distribution over words, inferred via Bayesian estimation (e.g., LDA) or its bilingual extensions.

Strengths:

Well-established baselines with transparent probabilistic interpretation of topics, useful for establishing benchmark coherence in bilingual OCR data (Balikas et al., 2021; , (Hao & Paul, 2020; , Smet et al., 2011; .
Strong theoretical grounding for cross-lingual variants (biLDA, PLTM) when parallel or comparable bilingual data are available, offering interpretable cross-language topic alignments (Balikas et al., 2021; , Heyman et al., 2015; , Smet et al., 2011; .
Simplicity and reproducibility; established pipelines exist for preprocessing, inference (Gibbs sampling, variational methods), and evaluation using coherence metrics (Hao & Paul, 2020; , Smet et al., 2011; .
Can be extended with bilingual priors or document links to encourage cross-language topic coherence in comparable corpora, aiding cross-lingual transfer without heavy external resources (Balikas et al., 2021; , Smet et al., 2011; , Chu et al., 2015).
Useful as a robust, language-agnostic baseline when working with OCR noise, because probabilistic topic assignments are relatively stable to moderate token distortions compared with high-variance neural methods (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; .
Weaknesses:

Susceptible to OCR noise when tokenization is corrupted; vocabulary skew and broken words degrade topic quality and coherence (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; .
Cross-language alignment relies on parallel or comparable corpora and may require additional resources (dictionaries, alignments) to achieve meaningful bilingual topics (Balikas et al., 2021; , Smet et al., 2011; , Chu et al., 2015).
Handling long ESG documents can dilute topics unless hierarchical or dynamic extensions are used; standard LDA operates at a single flat level (Moreno & Caminero, 2020; , Aryoseto et al., 2023; .
Tends to produce topics that are keyword-heavy and may ignore nuanced ABSA signals unless augmented with sentiment cues or taxonomy constraints (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Less effective in capturing fine-grained semantic nuance in code-switched Indonesian-English text without multilingual adaptations or embeddings Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
When to use:
Use LDA-style approaches as baseline topic extractors to establish a coherence and cross-language compatibility floor in OCR-derived ESG corpora. When bilingual comparable content is available, consider bilingual LDA variants (biLDA, PLTM) to align topics across Indonesian and English components; if no high-quality parallel data exist, augment with cross-lingual extensions or pair with embedding-based methods to improve cross-language coherence (Balikas et al., 2021; , Smet et al., 2011; , Chu et al., 2015).

3.2 Matrix factorization (NMF on TF-IDF)

Core idea:
Nonnegative matrix factorization (NMF) decomposes a document-by-term matrix (often TF-IDF) into two nonnegative factors: document-topic and topic-term, yielding latent topics as nonnegative combinations of terms.

Strengths:

Intuitive, fast, and scalable to large OCR-derived ESG corpora; does not require probabilistic generative assumptions, making it robust to certain noise patterns (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Works well with high-dimensional sparse data typical of long ESG reports after aggressive preprocessing; adaptable to bilingual vocabularies with careful normalization (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Produces highly interpretable topics as additive parts of documents, which can map cleanly to ESG taxonomy axes and ABSA facets with post-hoc labeling (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Can be applied on language-specific or multilingual TF-IDF representations to encourage cross-language topic alignment when bilingual signals are present (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Compatible with downstream clustering and visualization tools that support multi-scale ESG analysis, including section- or statement-level grouping (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Weaknesses:

Sensitive to preprocessing choices (tokenization, stopword removal, boilerplate filtering); OCR noise can disproportionately distort TF-IDF signals and degrade topic quality (Strien et al., 2020; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Lacks explicit probabilistic uncertainty estimates; interpreting topic significance in ESG contexts may require supplementary validation and human judgment (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Cross-language alignment is not intrinsic; bilingual coherence depends on how TF-IDF features are constructed across languages, which can be brittle under code-switching and OCR errors Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
May produce overlapping topics that require post-hoc disambiguation, especially in boilerplate-heavy ESG reports where similar terms recur across topics (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Not inherently suited for long-range hierarchical topic structure unless integrated with a multi-stage or hierarchical decomposition (Moreno & Caminero, 2020; , Aryoseto et al., 2023; .
When to use:
Apply NMF on TF-IDF as a strong, interpretable baseline for bilingual OCR ESG corpora, particularly when computational efficiency is prioritized or when probabilistic assumptions are undesirable. If cross-language coherence is essential, pair with bilingual embeddings or extend with polylingual constraints to improve alignment across Indonesian-English content (Li & Yao, 2020; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .

3.3 Embedding-based topic modelling (clustering + topic representation)

Core idea:
Embedding-based approaches map documents or tokens into a dense semantic space using contextual embeddings (e.g., transformers). Topics emerge by clustering in this space and deriving representative terms from cluster neighborhoods or centroid proximities (e.g., BERTopic, ETM).

Strengths:

Robust to moderate OCR noise when embeddings capture semantics beyond surface token sequences; leverages contextual information to stabilize topics in bilingual, noisy ESG data Zosa et al., 2021; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Naturally accommodates bilingual and code-switched text through multilingual or cross-lingual embeddings, enabling coherent cross-language topics without heavy dictionaries Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).
Produces semantically coherent topics with intuitive representations and allows dynamic, hierarchical, or multi-scale topic structures aligned with ESG taxonomy and ABSA cues Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).
Flexible integration with ABSA signals by using embeddings to link topics to sentiment-laden aspects (environmental impacts, governance practices) for interpretable insights (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Supports rapid experimentation and model iteration in implementation-driven reviews, enabling practical pipelines from raw OCRed text to deployment-ready topics Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; .
Weaknesses:

Dependence on high-quality embeddings; OCR noise or domain mismatch can degrade embeddings, affecting topic quality and stability in ESG contexts (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Requires careful handling of multilingual vocabulary and potential language imbalance; cross-lingual alignment quality hinges on embedding models and alignment strategies Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).
Computationally more intensive than BoW approaches; tuning hyperparameters for clustering (e.g., number of topics, distance metrics) is nontrivial in large ESG corpora Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; .
Interpretability may rely on post-hoc labeling of topics, which can be challenging when topics are highly nuanced or overlap across ESG axes (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Risk of degenerate topics if clustering is too fine-grained or if noise dominates the embedding space, necessitating robust preprocessing and noise-aware evaluation (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
When to use:
Use embedding-based topic modelling as a primary method when bilingual, OCR-derived ESG corpora demand semantic robustness and interpretability. This class is especially suitable when cross-language coherence is a priority, and when ABSA integration or taxonomy-aligned topic labeling is planned. It is often advantageous to pair embeddings with structured evaluation against ESG taxonomies to ensure practical usefulness Zosa et al., 2021; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Chu et al., 2015).

3.4 Neural topic models and hybrids

Core idea:
Neural topic models fuse neural representations with probabilistic or counting-based topic structures, including models that leverage variational autoencoders, attention mechanisms, or hybrid schemes combining probabilistic priors with neural encoders; hybrids may integrate embeddings, topic representations, and cross-lingual signals.

Strengths:

State-of-the-art performance in noisy or multilingual settings; neural components can learn robust representations that mitigate OCR noise and language mixing Chu et al., 2015), Hao & Paul, 2019; , Zafarian et al., 2015).
Enable flexible architectures that jointly optimize topic quality, cross-language alignment, and downstream ABSA or ESG taxonomy signals within a single objective, improving end-to-end interpretability Hao & Paul, 2019; , Zafarian et al., 2015).
Hybrids can incorporate external resources (dictionaries, ontologies) or structural cues (section boundaries, boilerplate segmentation) to stabilize topics in long ESG reports Chu et al., 2015), Hao & Paul, 2019; , Zafarian et al., 2015).
Facilitate dynamic or hierarchical topic modelling (topics over time or sections) to capture ESG-theme evolution and governance disclosures across years or document segments (Moreno & Caminero, 2020; , Aryoseto et al., 2023; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Amenable to end-to-end pipelines with OCR post-correction or noise-aware training objectives, enabling practical deployment in production ESG analytics workflows Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; , Hao & Paul, 2019; .
Weaknesses:

Data-hungry; neural models require substantial annotated or unsupervised data and careful regularization to avoid overfitting to OCR artifacts or boilerplate signals (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Training instability and sensitivity to hyperparameters; reproducibility and interpretability challenges are more pronounced than for classical models, especially in multilingual, long-document settings Hao & Paul, 2019; , Zafarian et al., 2015).
Higher computational costs; deployment in resource-constrained environments may be prohibitive without model compression or distillation strategies Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; .
Risk of learned biases or spurious cross-language correlations if the model overfits to boilerplate or nation-specific disclosures rather than material ESG topics (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; .
Evaluation complexity increases, as joint objectives require carefully designed metrics that capture coherence, stability, ABSA alignment, and taxonomy fidelity across languages (Strien et al., 2020; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; .
When to use:
Employ neural topic models and hybrids when the goal is end-to-end, cross-lingual topic discovery with strong integration to ABSA and ESG taxonomies, particularly for long, noisy ESG corpora with bilingual content. Use them in iterative experimentation alongside robust baselines (BoW/LDA, NMF) to assess gains in coherence, cross-language alignment, and downstream interpretability. Ensure evaluation plans include extrinsic checks against ESG taxonomy mappings and ABSA cues to avoid overfitting to noise or boilerplate signals (Balikas et al., 2021; , Chu et al., 2015), Hao & Paul, 2019; , Zafarian et al., 2015).

End of Section 3 notes:
Across these families, the choice of method should be guided by data availability (parallel/comparable bilingual resources), OCR quality, document length, and the intended downstream uses (taxonomy alignment, ABSA integration, governance insights). In practice, a pragmatic evaluation pipeline often starts with solid baselines (BoW LDA, TF-IDF-NMF), followed by embedding-based methods, and culminates in neural or hybrid models that couple topic discovery with interpretation-friendly signals and taxonomy mappings, all validated through both intrinsic coherence and extrinsic ESG interpretability criteria (Strien et al., 2020; , Zosa et al., 2021; , Hamdi et al., 2022; , Mutuvi et al., 2018; , Hämäläinen & Hengchen, 2019; , Fortuna & KHAERUZZAMAN, 2022; , Chu et al., 2015).
