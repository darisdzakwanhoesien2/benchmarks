Photojournalism is more than creating a durable image; it uses images in order to tell a story. A good photojournalist can tell a whole story with only a few pictures, pictures that stimulation emotion and thought. The interpretation of the spectator makes every story unique but is also a reflection of our own experiences and cultural beliefs. Good photojournalism not only creates emotion but suggests a message in our minds without immediate awareness of the spectator.

And this is what we want with the Permata Photojournalism Grant and the Erasmus Huis Fellowship to Amsterdam to one of the participants: to give experienced photojournalists the possibility to go beyond their daily life by challenge themselves with such an exciting theme like intangible heritage.

In our modern and global world intangible heritage is more important than ever. It represents the practices, expressions, knowledge and skills that people recognize as part of their cultural background. It is part of our identity, transmitted from generation to generation and provides us with a sense for cultural diversity and human nature. Photojournalism is one of the tools to preserve and pass on intangible heritage to our next generation.

That's the reason why I'm so thankful to Permata Bank and all the others who made this possible. They took their responsibility to preserve what is part of our existence. Thanks to all who worked so hard to realize this grant.

Media adalah salah satu jalan untuk memberikan pendidikan literasi keuangan bagi masyarakat, melalui penyebarluasan berita tentang layanan dan produk

With banking activity and financial institutions still concentrated in Indonesia's large cities, public access to banking remains dominated by urban communities. While many communities' approach to saving remains traditional, often in the form of cash stored in containers at home, hence use of digital mediums through smartphones opens ample opportunity for the banking world to enhance communities' financial literacy.
