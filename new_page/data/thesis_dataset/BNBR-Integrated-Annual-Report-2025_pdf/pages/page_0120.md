# PEREKONOMIAN INDONESIA: MENJAGA PERTUMBUHAN DAN STABILITAS DI TENGAH PERUBAHAN GLOBAL

Pada tahun 2025, perekonomian Indonesia tetap menunjukkan ketahanan yang solid meskipun pertumbuhan global melambat dan fragmentasi perdagangan meningkat, didukung oleh permintaan domestik yang kuat, kondisi keuangan yang stabil, serta pengelolaan makroekonomi yang prudensial (Laporan BI, 2025; IMF, 2025).

Nevertheless, conflicts in key energy-producing areas and ongoing trade disruptions occasionally reignited price pressures, particularly in fuel and transportation costs, complicating policy coordination and maintaining uncertainty around the pace of disinflation in both advanced and emerging economies worldwide.

Core inflation, excluding volatile food and energy components, showed gradual moderation in 2025 compared with the more persistent levels seen in 2024, reflecting easing wage pressures and softer service-sector demand. However, tight labor markets in several advanced economies continued to sustain relatively elevated service prices, preventing a rapid return to pre-pandemic inflation norms. Any renewed wage acceleration or supply constraints could still pose upside risks, keeping central banks cautious and financial markets sensitive to policy signals and inflation surprises.

Although economic activity has largely recovered from pandemic-era disruptions, global output in 2025 remains below earlier long-term projections, highlighting enduring structural constraints that became more visible after 2024. Aging populations, weak productivity growth, high debt levels, and uneven digital transformation continue to limit potential output, contributing to a prolonged period of moderate growth despite declining inflationary pressures.

In response, policymakers increasingly emphasize structural reforms aimed at enhancing productivity, improving labor market flexibility, and stimulating private investment. Compared with 2024, greater attention is now directed toward strengthening energy security, accelerating digitalization, and supporting climate transition initiatives through expanded infrastructure spending and industrial policies. Investments in green technologies, resilient supply chains, and human capital development are widely viewed as critical to sustaining long-term growth and ensuring economic stability in a more fragmented global environment.
