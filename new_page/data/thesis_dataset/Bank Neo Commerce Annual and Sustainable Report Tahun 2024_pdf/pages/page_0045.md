Otoritas Jasa Keuangan, dalam siaran pers pada tanggal 31 Desember 2024 menyatakan bahwa menutup tahun 2024, sektor perbankan Indonesia kembali menunjukkan daya tahan (resilient) di tengah ketidakpastian global dan tantangan ekonomi domestik. At the end of 2024, Bank Indonesia decided to maintain the BI-Rate at 6.00%, the Deposit Facility rate at 5.25%, and the Lending Facility rate at 6.75%. This decision is in line with the monetary policy direction to ensure inflation remains controlled within the target range, while supporting sustainable economic growth. The BI-Rate policy was also supported by foreign exchange market interventions, pro-market monetary operations, and sufficient foreign exchange reserves.

In a press release dated December 31, 2024, the Financial Services Authority (OJK) announced that, as the year 2024 closed, Indonesia's banking sector demonstrated resilience amid global uncertainties and domestic economic challenges. Through innovative and responsive strategies, the banking industry successfully sustained the stability of the financial system, supported economic activities, and reinforced stakeholder confidence. This has positioned the banking sector as a key pillar in the recovery and growth of the national economy.

Based on Banking Data Statistics as of December 2024, the banking sector's intermediation performance remained strong, as reflected in robust credit growth (commercial banks) of 10.39% year-on-year (yoy), an increase from 10.38% in the same period of the previous year. When categorized by loan type, investment loans recorded the highest growth at 13.62%, followed by consumer loans at 10.61%, and working capital loans at 8.35%. With 10.39% yoy credit growth in December 2024, the banking sector disbursed a total of Rp7,827 trillion in loans during this period.

On the other hand, third-party funds (TPF) in the banking sector grew by 4.48% year-on-year (yoy), reaching Rp8,837.2 trillion in 2024, higher than the 3.73% growth recorded in 2023.

Liquidity conditions in commercial banks also remained adequate, as reflected by the AL/NCD ratio of 112.87% and the AL/TPF ratio of 25.59%, both well above the regulatory thresholds of 50% and 10%, respectively. The capital position remained solid, with the Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) at 26.69%, despite a decline from the previous year, driven by a 9.89% yoy increase in Risk-Weighted Assets (RWA), in line with credit growth, which outpaced capital growth. Credit risk also improved, as reflected in the decline of the gross Non-Performing Loan (NPL) ratio to 2.08%, while the net NPL ratio remained stable at 0.74%.
