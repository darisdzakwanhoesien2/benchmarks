a. Transition Risk Scenario Analysis

In the transition risk scenario analysis, BTN incorporates relevant macroeconomic variables such as unemployment rates, energy prices (coal,

gas, oil), passenger numbers, and GDP growth. This simulation evaluates the impact on credit risk indicators, including Probability of Default (PD), Loss Given Default (LGD), and Non-Performing Loan (NPL).

The sensitivity analysis results indicate that transition risks lead to an increase in Probability of Default (PD) and Non-Performing Loan (NPL) in priority sectors such as energy and transportation, while Loss Given Default (LGD) increases in the property sector due to a decline in property values in high-risk areas. However, the NZ 2050 scenario simulation shows that BTN's Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) remains above the minimum threshold (10.8% compared to the minimum of 9.18%), indicating the Bank's ability to manage these risks.

b. Physical Risk Scenario Analysis

Physical risks involve damage caused by natural disasters, such as floods and fires, which impact the collateral value of assets and increase LGD. The focus areas are regions such as Bekasi, Bogor, and Makassar, which exhibit higher vulnerability to physical risks. Below are the Physical Risk Impacted

Areas for the Consumer Credit Property Ownership Sector (Conventional and Sharia).

From a financial impact perspective, direct losses due to physical risks are projected to reach Rp76.02 billion in 2024. Transition risks add implementation costs for NZE policies, including CAPEX of Rp59.67 billion and OPEX of Rp16.36 billion. Over the long term until 2050, the energy and infrastructure sectors are projected to experience significant impacts due to policy changes related to carbon pricing and fossil fuel energy costs.

The distribution of risk to the Bank's operations indicates that physical risk significantly impacts areas such as Bekasi, Bogor, and Makassar, particularly in the property sector and mortgage loans (KPR). Alternatively, transition risk primarily affects the energy sector due to rising carbon prices, with substantial impacts felt by debtors such as PLN.
