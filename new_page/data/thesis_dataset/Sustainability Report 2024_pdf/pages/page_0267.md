# Climate Risk Stress Testing (CRST)

As part of global objectives, since 2023, the Financial Services Authority (OJK) has issued policies on Climate Risk Stress Testing (CRST) for financial institutions. This policy aims to identify and manage risks related to climate change, including physical risks and transition risks towards a low-carbon economy. In response to this policy, BTN has taken positive steps by integrating the 2023 OJK CRST Guidelines into its risk management strategy. These guidelines serve as a reference for systematically assessing the potential impacts of

climate change. BTN is committed to supporting the transition to a low-carbon economy by adopting a science-based approach to climate risk analysis. Climate risk testing is conducted through scenario modeling and sensitivity analysis to identify potential impacts and mitigate possible risks.

BTN employs climate scenarios designed by the NGFS (Network for Greening the Financial System) and adjusts them to suit Indonesia's conditions.

Climate Risk Stress Testing (CRST) Matrix

The three main scenarios used include: Current Policies (CP), which reflect current policies without additional changes; Delayed Transition (DT), which depicts a delayed transition with drastic actions taken in the future; and Net Zero 2050 (NZ), which represents an ambitious scenario to achieve net zero carbon emissions by 2050. These scenarios include projections of macroeconomic

variables such as GDP, inflation, exchange rates, and interest rates, which are used to evaluate the impact of transition and physical risks on the bank's portfolio. The macroeconomic modeling phase utilizes variables such as inflation and exchange rates to calculate their impact on credit and securities risks.
