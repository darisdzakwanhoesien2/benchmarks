# Waste Management

## Waste Management

BTN generates various types of waste categorized into solid and liquid. This waste is further classified into hazardous and toxic waste and non-hazardous waste. The solid waste generated by the Bank includes office waste, electronic equipment, lamps, batteries, and old furniture. Meanwhile, the liquid waste generated includes used oil from operational vehicles and generators, as well as domestic wastewater from toilets and cafes. [GRI 3-3, 306-1]

BTN consistently makes efforts to manage all waste produced to minimize negative impacts on the environment and human health. Waste management refers to Law No. 18 of 2008 on Waste Management, Government Regulation No. 101 of 2014 on Hazardous Waste Management, and Government Regulation No. 74 on the Management of Hazardous and Toxic Substances. Specifically, for hazardous waste, BTN

collaborates with third parties licensed by the Ministry of Environment and the Ministry of Transportation for waste management. The Bank also ensures that waste management is carried out professionally and in compliance with regulations. [GRI 3-3, 306-2, 306-4]

In addition to waste management, BTN emphasizes 3R activities (Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle). Concrete steps include issuing appeals to prevent the use of disposable equipment and encouraging the reuse of organic waste from office waste to produce compost. BTN also provides waste bins on every floor of its office buildings to facilitate the waste management process. The Bank has prepared temporary hazardous waste storage facilities (TPS B3). [GRI 3-3, 306-2]
