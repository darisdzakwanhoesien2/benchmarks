# Energy Management

As a banking institution, energy plays a critical role in supporting operational activities and the technology used in providing daily financial services. BTN utilizes electricity to power and illuminate office facilities such as elevators, air conditioning, and photocopy machines. BTN also uses fuel to support operational activities,

including generators used as backup power sources. While energy consumption in the banking sector is not as extensive as in other business sectors, BTN recognizes that the risks arising from its activities still contribute to negative environmental impacts. [ORI 3-3]

# Energy Management Initiatives

As part of its commitment to the future, BTN actively conducts various energy efficiency initiatives as a strategic approach to reduce energy consumption. Significant energy usage primarily comes from elevator operations and air conditioning systems, both of which can be classified as SEU (Significant Energy Use). Therefore, BTN focuses on energy efficiency programs in the BTN Tower buildings, covering the efficient use of electrical equipment such as water heaters, air conditioners (chillers), and elevators. The initiatives carried out include the following: [ORI 3-3] [OJK F.7]

a. Operating 36 electric vehicles, consisting of 24 electric motorcycles and 12 electric cars, to support operational activities;
b. Developing Public Electric Vehicle Charging Stations (SPKLU) at the BTN Tower buildings as part of BTN's commitment to supporting the use of electric vehicles and reducing the carbon footprint;

c. Implementing policies to reduce electricity loads in offices after operational hours;
d. Utilizing a Building Automation System (BAS) to manage electrical equipment, including turning off lights, elevators, and cooling units to improve efficiency;
e. Turning off lighting in unused spaces through zone-based lighting controls;
f. Promoting energy-saving practices such as turning off lights when not in use and unplugging unused devices;
g. Using solar-powered water heaters for supplying hot water in the Executive restroom; and
