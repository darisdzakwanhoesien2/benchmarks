To strengthen resilience against credit risk, BTN applies credit risk measurement in alignment with the Basel III Reforms through a standardized approach as stipulated in SE OJK No. 24/SEOJK.03/2021 on Risk--Weighted Asset Calculation (ATMR). Decision--making authority regarding credit approval aligns with the Credit Committee's policies based on Special Policy Guidelines KK.9--D and the Governance Rules of the Board of Directors and Commissioners, including KK-1.Q on Authority to Approve Credit Decisions. The Bank also implements the Four Eyes Principle as a prudential measure by separating business and risk functions. Furthermore, credit analysis in the commercial and corporate segments has integrated ESG aspects to ensure a healthy and sustainable portfolio management, aligned with macroeconomic dynamics.

### Liquidity Risk

Liquidity risk arises when a bank fails to meet its maturing obligations from funding sources, either through cash flow or high-quality liquid assets, without disrupting operational activities or financial conditions. This risk can result from the inability to generate cash flow from productive assets, asset sales, fundraising activities, interbank transactions, or received loans. Therefore, managing liquidity risk is a critical aspect of maintaining the Bank's operational and financial stability.

In managing liquidity risk, the Bank refers to the Liquidity Risk Management Policy Guidelines, which are part of the broader Bank Risk Management Policy Guidelines. These guidelines encompass active oversight by the Board of Commissioners and Directors, liquidity risk management processes, and the formulation of policies, procedures, and risk limit settings. Liquidity risk measurement is carried out by the Treasury Division and the Risk Management & ESG Division following Technical Guidelines No. PT.8--F.4. The Bank employs four main methods to measure liquidity risk: stock-based analysis, maturity profile analysis, cash flow projections, and stress testing. Stress testing is conducted using Bank--Specific Crisis (BSC) and General Market Crisis (GMC) scenarios to measure the adequacy of available liquid assets.

The Bank regularly monitors daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly liquidity risks, evaluating exposures against internal limits, including Risk Appetite, Risk Tolerance, and Trigger Level Recovery Plans. Early Warning Indicator (EWI) monitoring is also conducted to identify potential liquidity risks early. In accordance with POJK No. 5 of 2024, dated March 27, 2024, regarding the Determination of Status and Resolution of Commercial Bank Issues, BTN monitors the Liquidity Coverage Ratio (LCR) and the Net Stable Funding Ratio (NSFR), as regulated under POJK 19 in the year 2024 and POJK 20 in the year 2024 These steps ensure that the Bank has a robust and appropriate liquidity management strategy that aligns with the latest regulatory requirements.

### Market Risk

Market risk represents a potential loss from the balance sheet and administrative account positions, including derivative transactions, due to overall market condition changes, such as interest rate risk, currency exchange rate risk, equity risk, and commodity risk. This risk may originate from both trading book and banking book positions. To manage market risk, BTN involves business units as well as the Enterprise & ESG Risk Management Division in the processes of identification, measurement, monitoring, and control of risk. Identification is conducted for each new product or activity that has the potential to generate market risk. Additionally, market risk management is regularly discussed in the Assets and Liabilities Committee (ALCO), focusing on banking book risks, particularly interest rates and exchange rates.

BTN measures interest rate and exchange rate risks through sensitivity analysis to assess the impact of market changes on profit (loss) and equity. For banking book positions, interest rate risk measurement uses the repricing gap method, as stipulated in Technical Guidelines No. PT.8--F.I, on Interest Rate Risk Management in the Banking Book (IRRBB). Additionally, in accordance with SE OJK No. 12/SEOJK.03/2018, BTN submits quarterly reports on IRRBB. To complement these measurements, BTN also conducts stress testing to evaluate resilience under extreme market changes. Stress testing results are formulated at the beginning of each year by the Enterprise & ESG Risk Management Division in collaboration with the Working Group Stress Testing within the Risk Division, referencing both regulatory and internal Bank scenarios.
