In the remuneration determination process, BTN collaborates with Willis Towers Watson to conduct benchmarking and/or salary surveys. The Bank has a policy to defer variable remuneration (Malus) or claw back variable remuneration already paid to officials categorized as Material Risk Takers (MRT) by considering various factors, including: [ORI 2-19]

1. The magnitude of losses or damage to the Bank's reputation.
2. The direct or indirect involvement of the relevant employee in actions or transactions conducted.

The salaries or honorariums for the Board of Commissioners and Directors are determined based on several factors, including:

1. The scale of the business;
2. Business complexity factors;
3. Inflation rate;
4. Financial condition and capability of the Bank;
5. Relevant factors, which must align with applicable laws and regulations; and
6. The composition of the salaries or honorariums for the Directors and the Board of Commissioners, which are determined as follows:

a. The salary of the President Director is determined based on internal guidelines set by the minister;
b. The salary of the Vice President Director is set at 95% of the President Director's salary;
c. The salaries of other Directors are set at 85% of the President Director's salary;
d. The honorarium for the President Commissioner is set at 45% of the President Director's salary;
e. The honorarium for the Vice President Commissioner is set at 42.5% of the President Director's salary; and
f. The honorarium for other Commissioners is set at 90% of the President Commissioner's honorarium.

The determination of remuneration at BTN is not only based on individual roles and responsibilities but is also influenced by the achievement of predefined

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). These KPIs serve as metrics to assess the extent to which strategic and operational corporate goals are achieved. One of the key parameters in the KPIs for Directors is the aspect of sustainability, which focuses on achieving long-term goals that support the company's social, economic, and environmental continuity. The remuneration package and facilities received by the Board of Commissioners and Directors include a remuneration structure comprising salary, short-term incentives, long-term incentives, pensions, benefits, and one-off payments (such as recruitment or retention bonuses). [ORI 2-19]

BTN provides short-term incentives as immediate rewards for employee performance achievements within a short period, typically one year or less. These incentives are governed by Special Policy No. 3-B, on Remuneration, which refers to POJK No. 45/POJK.03/2015. The short-term incentive program includes various forms of rewards, such as cash bonuses, performance-based salary increases, profit-sharing, and other financial incentives designed to motivate employees to achieve corporate targets within a relatively short time frame.

In addition to short-term incentives, BTN also offers long-term incentives as a recognition of employees' sustained contributions over multiple years. These incentives are regulated under KK.3-D, on Compensation for the Board of Directors, Board of Commissioners, Senior Executive Vice Presidents (SEVP), and Supporting Organs of the Board of Commissioners, as well as Special Policy KK.3-B, on Remuneration, which aligns with Minister of SOEs Regulation No. Per-13/MBU/09/2021, and POJK No. 45/POJK.03/2015. Long-term incentives may include shares, cash rewards, and other forms of recognition aimed at encouraging employees to focus on the company's long-term objectives while ensuring their retention and loyalty to the organization.
