# Compliance in Environmental and Social Management [GRI 2-25] [SASB FN-CB-410A.2]

The Bank holds direct and indirect responsibility for managing environmental and social impacts potentially caused by debtor activities. Any non-compliance in managing environmental and social impacts may affect the Bank's credit risk and reputation. This responsibility aligns with the Bank's role as an intermediary institution.

In its funding and credit disbursement processes, BTN consistently implements environmental risk management. This approach supports the implementation of sustainable finance principles and complies with the POJK Number 40/POJK.03/2019 on the Assessment of Asset Quality of Commercial Banks, issued by the Financial Services Authority (OJK) in 2019, which states that assessments of business prospects, as part of credit quality evaluation, should include considerations of the efforts made by debtors to preserve the environment. [FS1]

The commercial credit portfolio has become a key priority in promoting BTN's growth. Therefore, the Bank has developed specific policies and procedures for disbursing commercial credit while considering environmental and social dimensions. These measures aim to ensure that commercial credit disbursements remain aligned with prudent banking principles and good corporate governance.

In KU.1 - General Policy on Bank Lending or Financing, the following are following are regulated as part of legality proof for projects: [FS1, FS2] [SASB FN-CB-240a.3]

1. Land allotment;
2. Site plan authorized by the relevant authority;
3. Building Permit;
4. Proof of ownership;
5. Copy of SPPT;
6. Proof of road access ownership;
7. Flood-free certification;
8. Certification from the local water company (PDAM); and
9. Environmental Feasibility Certificate or Environmental Management Efforts and/or Environmental Impact Analysis (AMDAL).

BTN has begun to apply credit risk assessments by considering AMDAL results before granting credit decisions for a project. This is due to AMDAL's significance as a critical requirement for licensing needed during the decision-making process for planned business activities. The AMDAL document can also provide an evaluation of potential impacts arising from business activities on the surrounding environment. AMDAL preparation is carried out during the planning stage of projects that are anticipated to impact the environment, such as abiotic, biotic, and cultural aspects. Legal regulations for AMDAL in Indonesia are stipulated under Government Regulation No. 27 of 2012 on Environmental Permits.

In addition to considering AMDAL documents, the Bank also conducts on-site monitoring or verification. This ensures that prospective debtors comply with all applicable environmental regulations, reflecting prudential principles in credit risk management.

BTN also embraces the initiative "one home, one tree" as part of its commitment to environmental and social sustainability. Through this initiative, every house built is required to include a green area with tree planting. Furthermore, every housing project is equipped with social and public facilities. [FS1, FS2, FS5]

To ensure the effectiveness of this initiative, the Bank regularly conducts audits, including identifying follow-up actions in the event of breaches. These audits are carried out annually by the Internal Audit Unit. During the general audit of Branch Offices, auditors will use sampling techniques to perform inspections and evaluations of compliance with existing policies. If discrepancies are found, auditors will investigate their causes and provide recommendations for corrective actions. In addition, audited Branch Offices are also required to commit to necessary improvements, which will subsequently be monitored regularly. [OJK F.10] [FS9]
