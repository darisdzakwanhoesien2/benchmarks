Based on the Bank Indonesia Board of Directors Decree No. 27/55/KEP/DIR dated 23 September 1994, BTN was authorized to operate as a foreign exchange bank. In 2002, BTN was appointed as a commercial bank focused on housing financing. The Bank received approval from Bank Indonesia to conduct general banking activities based on Sharia principles through Letter No. 6/1350/DPbS dated 15 December 2004. Subsequently, on 14 February 2005, BTN began Sharia banking operations by opening its first Sharia branch in Jakarta-Harmoni.

In 2009, BTN successfully carried out Indonesia's first housing loan securitization through an asset-backed securities collective investment contract (KIK-EBA). On 17 December 2009, BTN conducted its Initial Public Offering (IPO) and was listed on the Indonesia Stock Exchange. In 2015, BTN was designated a key player in supporting the government's "One Million Homes for the People" program.

In 2017, BTN continued toward digital banking by accelerating digital transformation across all services, including launching Smart Branches equipped to

meet customers' needs, such as obtaining banking information, communication, transactions, registration, and account openings. By 2019, BTN had solidified its position as the fifth-largest bank and a Housing Market Maker. In 2022, BTN issued new shares for the second time since 2012. This effort aimed to expand credit growth and strengthen capital to support BTN's mission to become the Best Mortgage Bank in Southeast Asia.

BTN's achievements in 2024 as an ESG Champion have gained global recognition. This is evident from two international awards received: Mortgage Product of the Year-ESG Winner and Highly Acclaimed for Best ESG Initiative. These awards acknowledge BTN's innovations in ESG and sustainable finance initiatives that promote circular economic growth. The success reflects BTN's continued commitment to sustainability by integrating environmental, social, and governance (ESG) aspects into its operations and strengthening its role in supporting sustainable and circular economic development in Indonesia.
