# Customer Data Privacy and Security

In managing the implementation of information technology (IT), BTN has established IT governance from the management, planning, development, operational infrastructure, and security levels of its information technology systems. The bank also conducts regular periodic reviews of IT governance by disclosing them in the General Policy on Information Technology, Data Management, and Digitalization. This policy regulates IT governance at a high level.

BTN has also developed derivative policies in the form of specific policies that focus more on technical aspects and provide detailed guidelines for policy implementation. These specific policies aim to provide more structured implementation and supervision guidance according to operational needs. They cover the business unit and subsidiaries of BTN, including the entire data cycle, from collection, processing, storage, to data disposal. BTN recognizes the importance of information technology (IT) as a key element in ensuring smooth and efficient business operations. Given that IT policies cover a broad and complex range of aspects, the Bank divides IT governance into three main domains, each with a distinct role and focus. First, “Change the Bank”, which focuses on IT system planning and development. Second, “Run the Bank”, which focuses on IT operations and ensuring the reliability of the infrastructure and systems used by both the bank and customers. Lastly, “Secure the Bank”, which focuses on maintaining the security of the Bank's systems and applications to protect them from IT security threats. [GRI 3--3]

With the rapid advancement of information technology and increasing customer demand for more practical banking transactions through digitalization, significant challenges have emerged in maintaining security within the “Secure the Bank” domain. One of the biggest challenges is ensuring that digital services remain secure from increasingly complex threats, such as cyberattacks. BTN acknowledges that while digitalization enhances customer convenience, it also makes services more vulnerable to potential threats due to the shift in customer preferences from conventional banking channels (such as branches or face-to-face services) to digital platforms.
