# BTN Portfolio Guideline

As a financial institution, BTN is committed to continuously strengthening credit risk management in line with economic growth and prevailing monetary policies. BTN has implemented the Portfolio Guideline (PG) to support business and financial stability. The Portfolio Guideline is a strategic tool aimed at effectively managing credit concentration risks through measured portfolio diversification.

Through the implementation of the Portfolio Guideline, BTN seeks to maintain asset quality by directing credit allocation to priority sectors with sustainable growth prospects. This initiative not only supports business growth but also contributes to Environmental, Social, and Governance (ESG) risk mitigation. BTN ensures that the Portfolio Guideline is implemented in an integrated and consistent manner with its credit risk management strategy. As a result, the Portfolio Guideline has become a key component in balancing credit growth and risk management, aligning with BTN's sustainability principles.

With the following objectives:

Managing credit concentration risks through measured portfolio diversification based on industry research, ESG risk analysis, and sector risk assessment.

Encouraging diversified and sustainable credit growth by aligning allocations with priority industries.

Providing clear guidelines for priority sectors to support responsible credit growth.

Monitoring and managing sectoral risks within the prioritized industries.

1. In implementing ESG principles, the Portfolio Guideline aligns with Technical Guidance Number PT. 8-H.3. The issuance of the Portfolio Guideline serves as a tool to manage credit concentration risk. BTN has identified several industrial sectors as a reference for credit allocation, considering ESG aspects, prudential principles, and sound credit management. This approach ensures alignment between ESG aspects. The designated sectors include:

a. Environmentally friendly transportation, public transportation, warehousing, and communications;
b. Financial intermediaries;
c. Healthcare services and social activities;
d. Educational services;
