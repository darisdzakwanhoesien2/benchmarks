In 2024, global headline inflation moderated in many regions as the easing of last year's steep energy price increases began to take hold, even as production adjustments by key OPEC+ economies—and renewed supply disruptions—reintroduced volatility in energy markets (IMF, 2024; J.P. Morgan, 2024). However, geopolitical risks, particularly with tensions in the Middle East, continued to influence retail fuel prices by curbing supplies and triggering localized inflation rebounds in both advanced and emerging markets. Spillover from this instability complicates inflation and monetary policy prospects in various global economic regions.

Underlying inflation measures, which exclude the volatile food and energy sectors, have shown only modest declines in 2024. Persistent pressure in the services sector—where wages are the primary cost driver—has kept core inflation sticky, meaning that any unforeseen wage increases or labor market shortages could lead to unexpected upward inflation surprises, convoluting future market expectation and monetary policy (J.P. Morgan, 2024).
