2. Build the emissions hotspot map — identify major sources (operations, logistics, purchased energy).  
3. Establish governance and accountability — assign ownership, KPIs, budgeting.  
4. Set science‑aligned targets and a transition plan — near‑ & long‑term targets, milestones.  
5. Execute “no‑regret” efficiency measures — energy efficiency across buildings, transport.  
6. Decarbonize purchased electricity — shift to low‑carbon power via PPAs, certificates.  
7. Electrify key end uses — replace fossil equipment with electric alternatives.  
8. Reduce process and fuel emissions — process improvements, methane detection, fuel switching.  
9. Redesign products/services for lower lifecycle emissions — product design, circularity.  
10. Build a supplier decarbonization program — supplier engagement, targets, measurement.  
11. Decarbonize logistics & distribution — modal shifts, fuel alternatives.  
12. Embed circularity in operations & value chain — reuse, remanufacture, material lifecycle.  
13. Address “hard‑to‑abate” emissions with innovation — pilots, partnerships for technology.  
14. Manage residual emissions transparently — reporting, monitoring, clear assumptions.  
15. Neutralize residuals with high‑quality removals — offsets/removals as last resort, prioritized transparently.  
16. Assure, disclose, continuously improve — external assurance, progress reporting.  
17. Maintain net‑zero through operations & capital allocation — governance to avoid rebound.

Notes: roadmap prioritises in‑house abatement first, supplier & product interventions next, removals/offsets last; governance, measurement and disclosure run across all steps.

- 2010s: TCFD recommendations, sustainability reporting dialogues and national/regional initiatives.
- Recent rapid consolidation: emergence of ISSB, EU CSRD/CS3DD, IFRS S2, TCFD uptake, and sectoral standards (2020–2026).
