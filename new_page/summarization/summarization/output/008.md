Section 9. Research Gaps and Future Directions

Reference-free evaluation for ESG: There is a clear need for robust, reference-independent evaluation paradigms that can assess factual fidelity, grounding, and policy-compliance of ESG summaries without relying solely on reference summaries. This includes developing task-tailored metrics that measure alignment with primary sources, evidentiary density, and auditability, as well as benchmarks that simulate regulator and investor review workflows. Such reference-free signals could leverage NLI, QA-based sanity checks, and grounding diagnostics to detect hallucinations and misattributions in ESG contexts (Fei et al., 2022; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020), Qi et al., 2022), and align with emerging calls for explainable, evidence-grounded NLP in governance settings [59], [63].

Numeric and table faithfulness checks: ESG disclosures encode critical KPI values, baselines, units, and timeframes within tables and figures. Current generation pipelines often struggle to preserve exact numbers, which undermines materiality judgments and regulatory compliance. Future work should fortify automated numeric-grounding pipelines, including cross-table consistency checks, unit normalization, and automated verification against source KPI tables or data dashboards. This remains an area with limited validated, standardized benchmarks in Indonesian ESG corpora (Fei et al., 2022; , Maitama et al., 2020), Qi et al., 2022).

Multilingual domain adaptation for Indonesian ESG: Indonesian disclosures increasingly blend Indonesian and English, and incorporate international standards (GRI, POJK, SPOTT). Multilingual adaptation for ABSA-aware summarization needs domain-specific vocabularies, code-switching handling, and robust cross-language grounding. Research directions include targeted pretraining on ESG corpora, multilingual ABSA that preserves pillar-specific signals, and retrieval-augmented generation that can navigate language-switch boundaries while maintaining evidentiary anchors (Prabhune et al., 2024; , [59], [60], [61], [62], [64].

End-to-end error attribution across OCR → extraction → summarization: A central practical gap is tracing errors end-to-end—from OCR misreads and table-structure misinterpretations through to extraction errors and abstractive misstatements. Building comprehensive provenance chains and error budgets for each pipeline stage, along with diagnostic dashboards that isolate which component contributed to a given factual discrepancy, will improve transparency, debugging efficiency, and trustworthiness in ESG summaries (Fei et al., 2022; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020), Section 8 notes on provenance and grounding.

Human-centered utility evaluation: Beyond technical fidelity, there is a need to quantify human-centered usefulness for investors, auditors, and corporate governance teams. This includes evaluating how summaries support decision-making, regulatory review efficiency, and the perceived trustworthiness of outputs. Standardized, domain-specific human studies—covering faithfulness, coverage, usefulness, and auditability—should accompany automated metrics to inform design choices and ensure outputs meet practitioner needs (Viñán-Ludeña & Campos, 2024; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020), Section 5.

Benchmarking ecosystems with cross-domain transfer: To accelerate progress, it is beneficial to adopt benchmark environments that reflect ESG-specific tasks (e.g., evidence-grounded ABSA-aware summarization) and enable cross-domain transfer learning. Concepts from Turing-test-inspired benchmarks and multilingual ESL benchmarks suggest evaluating not only accuracy but also grounding, robustness to multilingual input, and resistance to hallucinations across domains and languages [65], [66], [67]. Such benchmarks can promote reproducibility, fairness, and reliability in ESG NLP research.

Open governance and reproducibility practices: Given the governance-critical nature of ESG outputs, future research should standardize reproducible workflows, including data provenance, versioned corpora, model weights with clear licensing, and transparent evaluation dashboards. Section 8 outlines a repo-integrated blueprint; extending this with community-driven guidelines and open auditing mechanisms would enhance credibility and applicability in real-world Indonesian ESG contexts (Chebolu et al., 2023), Section 8 .
