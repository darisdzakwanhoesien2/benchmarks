Key takeaways

Method landscape: The field recognizes a spectrum of summarization methods for ESG texts, ranging from extractive approaches (lead-based, graph-based TextRank, supervised extractive models) to abstractive generation (neural seq2seq with pretraining such as BART/PEGASUS) and long-document strategies (hierarchical encoders, retrieval-augmented generation). Hybrid approaches that combine extractive grounding with constrained abstractive generation are increasingly favored for ESG contexts where evidence-anchorability and pillar-structure are essential Section 3; Section 5; Section 6.

Evaluation needs: ESG summarization demands beyond standard automatic metrics. Overlap-based metrics (ROUGE) provide baseline comparability but are insufficient for ESG due to the need for evidentiary grounding, numerical fidelity, and cross-document consistency. Semantic similarity adds paraphrase sensitivity but still lacks guarantees about source alignment. Faithfulness, through NLI/QA-based checks and classifier-grounded verifications, is critical, and human evaluation focusing on faithfulness, coverage, usefulness, and auditability remains indispensable. A benchmark mindset with reproducibility dashboards and provenance needs is essential for credible deployment Section 5; Section 6; Section 8.

Faithfulness priority: Across sections, faithfulness is elevated as the central constraint for ESG outputs. This includes ensuring exact KPI values, correct regulatory references, and traceable citations to source passages. End-to-end error attribution (OCR → extraction → summarization) and explicit grounding are highlighted as practical imperatives for trustworthy ESG summaries. The proposed ABSA-aware framing places assertion and evidence in a structured, audit-friendly manner, enabling traceability from aspect signals to source documents Section 6; Section 7.

ABSA-aware framing: ABSA is presented not only as a sentiment extraction tool but as a content-planning and auditing scaffold. ABSA outputs map to pillar-specific aspects, informing what must be included, what evidence is required, and where provenance should be anchored. This framing supports pillar-structured outputs, explicit evidence anchors, and cross-document grounding, enabling both readability and regulator-friendly audibility. The ABSA-driven design yields multiple summary units (record-level, company-level, comparative, chapter-ready) each carrying rich metadata about provenance, aspect coverage, and tone/commitment distribution, and supports three strategy sets (extractive, constrained abstractive, hybrid) chosen by context Section 7.

Open challenges and future directions (implied): There is a need for reference-free evaluation tailored to ESG, robust numeric/table faithfulness checks, robust multilingual domain adaptation for Indonesian ESG, end-to-end error attribution across OCR → extraction → summarization, and formal human-centered utility evaluation. These directions point toward a more trustworthy, governance-aware, and reproducible ESG NLP ecosystem Section 9.

Thesis statement

Evidence-grounded, auditable summarization is the practical path for ESG: by uniting ABSA-informed content planning with explicit evidence grounding and a constrained, auditable pipeline, Indonesian ESG disclosures can be transformed into trustworthy, decision-ready summaries that meet regulatory expectations and stakeholder needs. This framework prioritizes faithfulness and provenance, accommodates multilingual and OCR-noise realities, and supports reproducible, human-centered evaluation throughout the lifecycle of ESG reporting.
