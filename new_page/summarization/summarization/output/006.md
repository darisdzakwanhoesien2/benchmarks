Section 7. ABSA-Aware, Evidence-Grounded Summarization for ESG (Proposed Framing)

This section proposes a cohesive framing for Indonesian ESG disclosures that leverages aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) outputs as both a content-planning instrument and an audit scaffold, enabling summaries that are pillar-structured, evidence-grounded, and auditable. We define summary units and metadata, and present a three-strategy set (extractive, constrained abstractive, hybrid) with guidance on when to deploy each. The framing emphasizes traceability from ASBA-like aspect signals to source evidence, supporting decision-making by investors, regulators, and governance teams.

7.1 Why ABSA Matters for ESG Summarization

ABSA as a governance-aware lens

ABSA dissects text along aspects and pillars (Environment, Social, Governance) and attributes sentiment or evaluative stance to each aspect. This granularity aligns with ESG materiality and enables summaries to reflect not only whether a claim is positive or negative, but which facet it concerns and with what degree of confidence. In Indonesian ESG disclosures, where narratives are interwoven with KPI data and normative statements, ABSA provides a structured blueprint for capturing both qualitative judgments and quantitative evidence around each pillar [59], [60], [61], [62].
ABSA outputs can function as a content plan: each identified aspect (e.g., “GHG emissions intensity,” “board independence,” “stakeholder engagement”) with an associated sentiment and confidence acts as a contract for what needs to be included in the final summary and what evidence will be required to support it. This content-plan role supports auditability by making explicit which claims must be anchored to source passages, tables, or governance documents [59], [60], [61].
ABSA as an audit scaffold

By mapping sentences to aspects, ABSA naturally yields provenance trails: each summarized claim can be traced back to one or more source fragments. When ABSA outputs include provenance metadata (source document, section, figure/table reference), the summarizer can enforce explicit citations, enabling cross-verification and regulatory review. This scaffolding is crucial for ESG contexts where stakeholders demand verifiable evidence for material claims and targets (Fei et al., 2022; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020), Qi et al., 2022).
ABSA-informed content-plans support constraint mechanisms: if a claim about an environmental KPI lacks explicit source anchoring, the pipeline can flag it for verification or rejection, thereby reducing hallucinations and misattributions in high-stakes ESG outputs (Fei et al., 2022; , Maitama et al., 2020).
7.2 Summary Unit Design

We define a hierarchy of summary units to support flexible decision contexts, each with required metadata that anchors claims to evidence and preserves auditability.

Record-level unit

Pillar coverage: typically Environment; may include Governance context if the record links to governance actions.
Required metadata:
Provenance: source document name, year, section/table reference, data table row/column.
Aspect/pillar coverage: explicit mapping to an ESG aspect and pillar.
KPI details: metric name, units, baseline, year, target if present.
Tone/commitment distribution: sentiment about performance and any stated commitments.
Company-level unit

Description: A compact synthesis for a given company, aggregating multiple record-level items into a coherent narrative (e.g., “Environment initiatives reduced emissions intensity by X%; governance reforms expanded board independence; social programs intensified stakeholder engagement”).
Pillar coverage: Environment, Social, Governance with explicit cross-pillar balance.
Required metadata:
Provenance: compilation sources (list of documents) with relative weighting.
Aspect coverage: set of identified aspects across pillars.
Cross-year trajectory indicators: direction/magnitude per pillar.
Tone distribution: overall sentiment per pillar and per aspect.
Comparative unit

Description: Head-to-head comparison across companies or across years, highlighting material differences and evidence anchors (e.g., “Company A vs Company B: governance disclosures baseline and improvements; environmental KPI gaps”).
Pillar coverage: balanced across Environment, Social, Governance to enable side-by-side materiality judgment.
Required metadata:
Provenance: sources for each compared item, with explicit cross-linking to the counterpart.
Comparable KPI definitions: ensure units and baselines are aligned.
Confidence and grounding metadata: whether each claim is cross-validated across sources.
Chapter-ready unit

Description: Coherent, section-length summaries suitable for ESG chapter narratives in reports, investor briefs, or regulatory submissions, integrating multiple record-, company-, and comparative-level insights into a structured narrative.
Pillar coverage: full ESG spectrum with clearly delineated subsections per pillar and an evidence appendix.
Required metadata:
Provenance bundle: full citation ledger for each claim.
Chapter structure metadata: headings, subsections, and planned paragraph boundaries.
Tone and risk framing: explicit statements of positive progress, residual risk, and commitments.
Metadata schema (covering provenance, aspect/pillar coverage, and tone/commitment distribution)

Provenance: documentid, year, sectionreference, figure/tableid, lineitem_id (if applicable).
Aspect/pillar coverage: pillar (Environment/Social/Governance), aspectlabel, subaspect, sentimentlabel, confidencescore.
Tone/commitment distribution: overall tone (positive/neutral/negative), commitmentflag (stated target or policy), riskflag (e.g., disclosure of risk or uncertainty), and a compact distribution vector across pillars.
7.3 Strategy Set for ABSA-Aware Summarization

We propose three primary strategies, with guidance on their interoperability, constraints, and ideal use cases in Indonesian ESG contexts.

Strategy A: Extractive ABSA-Driven Grounding (Extractive)

Description: Build the summary exclusively from verbatim source passages that ABSA has identified as salient for each aspect, preserving exact wording and citations.
When to prefer: regulatory audits demanding exact quotes, high-stakes KPI reporting where numerical fidelity must be untouched, or when OCR noise is mild and evidence anchors are clearly legible.
Strengths: maximum traceability, zero generation-time hallucinations, straightforward auditability.
Limitations: limited fluency and coherence; potential redundancy; requires robust post-processing to form readable chapters or briefs.
Strategy B: Constrained Abstractive Summarization (Constrained Abstractive)

Description: Use abstractive generation guided by explicit content plans and citation anchors; enforce constraints so that every claim is grounded to a source fragment via citation tags, and numerical values are verified against attached tables.
When to prefer: when narratives must be user-friendly, cross-entity comparisons are needed, or pillar-aligned coherence is prioritized, provided there is a strong grounding mechanism.
Strengths: fluent, cohesive, presentation-ready summaries; preserves evidence via anchors; can synthesize across sources while preserving attribution.
Limitations: risk of semantic drift or hallucination if constraints are too weak; requires robust grounding supervision and adjudication for numerical data.
Strategy C: Hybrid ABSA-Enhanced Grounding (Hybrid)

Description: Combine extractive ABSA-driven anchors with a constrained abstractive layer and a retrieval-augmented grounding component. ABSA outputs define the evidence landscape; a retriever fetches corroborating passages; abstractive generation weaves the narrative while maintaining strict citations and cross-document grounding.
When to prefer: cross-document, pillar-wide ESG briefs requiring both strong grounding and narrative quality; multi-year, cross-company comparisons; or where OCR noise is present but enough high-confidence passages exist to support synthesis.
Strengths: balanced fidelity and readability; robust evidence grounding; scalable to long documents.
Limitations: more complex pipeline, higher engineering overhead, and the need for careful calibration of grounding signals and retrieval quality.
Guiding principles for strategy selection

Start with the ABSA-derived content plan to identify required aspects, sources, and evidence anchors before generation.
Prefer extractive when auditability and exact quotation are paramount; switch to constrained abstractive when readability and cross-source synthesis are needed; employ hybrid when both grounding and narrative coherence are required.
Ensure all three strategies produce outputs with explicit provenance, pillar coverage, and traceable evidence anchors to satisfy ESG trust requirements.
Reference alignment

The ABSA-informed framing aligns with literature on ABSA’s role in domain-specific sentiment understanding and its utility for content planning and explainability in NLP systems [59], [60], [61], [62]. It also resonates with work on explainable AI in ESG contexts that advocates grounding, cross-document evidence trails, and user-centered interpretability [59], [63].
