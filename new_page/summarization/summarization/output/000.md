Section 1. Introduction

What summarization is and why it matters in operational contexts
Summarization in natural language processing refers to the automatic production of concise, coherent representations of longer texts, preserving essential claims, evidence, and context. In operational settings, effective summarization accelerates decision-making, risk assessment, and regulatory compliance by transforming voluminous disclosures into timely, decision-ready briefs for investors, managers, and policymakers. Recent advances enable end-to-end pipelines that move from content ingestion to targeted summaries, supporting tasks such as monitoring, auditing, and governance oversight. In ESG-related domains, concise summaries must retain salient claims, quantitative metrics, and evidentiary cues to support audits and trust-based evaluations, making faithful summarization not only a convenience but a governance imperative (Kaur & Sharma, 2024; , Preetha et al., 2023; , Zhang et al., 2024; , Ding et al., 2022; .

Why Indonesian ESG disclosures (sustainability reports) are a special case
Indonesian sustainability disclosures exhibit a distinctive fusion of narrative discussion, numeric KPIs, and compliance-oriented language. They routinely integrate qualitative narratives about environmental and social initiatives with quantified performance indicators (e.g., emissions data, energy intensity, stakeholder engagement metrics) and explicit references to regulatory or reporting standards. This mixed modality content creates unique challenges for summarization: preserving numerical fidelity, maintaining traceability to cited evidence (policy texts, certifications, or disclosed data tables), and conveying regulatory or normative commitments in a compressed form. The Indonesian context also features heterogenous reporting practices across listed companies, variations in governance disclosures, and language considerations (Indonesian primarily, with corporate English summaries in some reports), all of which impact how information is extracted, organized, and presented in summaries (Fei et al., 2022; , Qi, 2023; , Alshaikh et al., 2024), Yao et al., 2020).

Why common benchmark assumptions (news datasets, reference summaries) do not transfer cleanly
Standard benchmarking in summarization often relies on newswire corpora or well-formed reference summaries that assume relatively clean, single-domain text and stable factual grounding. ESG disclosures, by contrast, pose several transfer-friction issues:

Multimodal and structured content: ESG reports combine prose with tables, charts, and compliance statements, requiring surface and structural understanding beyond plain text (Fei et al., 2022; , Maitama et al., 2020).
Mixed evidence grounding: Claims are supported by primary documents, certifications, and governance artifacts; effective summaries must anchor to sources to support auditability and trust (Fei et al., 2022; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020).
OCR-induced noise: Many Indonesian disclosures originate from scanned documents, historical PDFs, and redacted layouts, introducing recognition errors, layout ambiguities, and multilingual elements that degrade extraction and factual consistency if not explicitly addressed (Fei et al., 2022; , Degife & Lin, 2024; , Chebolu et al., 2023).
ESG-specific materiality and regulatory framing: Summaries must reflect ESG materiality and the evidentiary basis of claims (e.g., governance structures, risk disclosures, certifications) rather than generic factual accuracy alone, demanding evaluation paradigms that capture alignment with ESG evidence bases (Fei et al., 2022; , Qi, 2023; , Alshaikh et al., 2024).
These factors imply that benchmarking on conventional news-like datasets and generic reference summaries may produce overoptimistic or inapplicable estimates of performance for Indonesian ESG summarization tasks. A domain-sensitive evaluation suite that emphasizes factual grounding, source citation, and cross-document consistency is required to reflect real-world use cases in Indonesian ESG reporting (Fei et al., 2022; , Zhang et al., 2023; , Lv et al., 2023; , Maitama et al., 2020).
Preview of the structure of the paper

Methods: We trace the progression from extractive to abstractive summarization approaches, highlighting their suitability and limitations for OCR-noisy ESG disclosures, and discuss evaluation paradigms that center factual grounding and evidence consistency.
Datasets and domain: We characterize Indonesian ESG disclosure corpora, including reporting practices, multilingual content, and the integration of narratives with KPI data, and discuss OCR-quality considerations and cross-document evidence trails.
Evaluation: We examine existing evaluation metrics and propose ESG-aware criteria emphasizing faithfulness to source passages, numerical fidelity, and cross-source verifiability, including the role of citation anchors.
Faithfulness: We emphasize the distinction between textual faithfulness and factual faithfulness, surveying methods to assess abstractive outputs for consistency with primary disclosures and regulatory language.
ABSA-aware framing: We present an ABSA-informed perspective on summarization that identifies ESG aspects (Environment, Social, Governance), aligns sentiments or evaluations with explicit evidence, and ensures traceable, aspect-level accountability.
Implementation blueprint: We outline an end-to-end blueprint for building ABSA-aware, evidence-grounded summarization pipelines tailored to Indonesian ESG disclosures, including OCR-resilient modules, evidence grounding, and evaluation components.
Gaps and open challenges: We conclude with gaps in data, methodologies, and governance integration, and propose concrete directions for future research and practice.
Synthesis with the literature
Across the relevant strands—ABSA for fine-grained aspect–sentiment understanding, robustness to OCR and multilingual text, and the need for evidence-grounded summaries in ESG contexts—the literature converges on a common theme: trustworthy, domain-aware summarization requires more than fluent generation; it requires explicit grounding, traceability, and alignment with domain-specific evidence. Works addressing ABSA robustness and end-to-end ABSA pipelines, as well as studies examining ESG disclosures and rating dynamics in Indonesian contexts, provide the conceptual and empirical foundation for the proposed approach. In particular, recent studies on ABSA robustness to context perturbations and multilingual or domain-specific ABSA methods inform the design of OCR-resilient, evidence-grounded summarization systems, while Indonesian ESG-focused analyses motivate the evaluation criteria and evidentiary anchoring necessary for trustworthy summaries (Kaur & Sharma, 2024; , Almasaud & Al-Baity, 2023; , Jin et al., 2020; , Ma et al., 2020; , Verma & Lee, 2018; , Ding et al., 2022; , Banko & Vanderwende, 2004; , Le et al., 2023; , Kurniawan & Louvan, 2018; , Cahyawijaya et al., 2021; , Yulianti et al., 2023; , Mutasodirin & Prasojo, 2021; , Maylawati et al., 2023; , Cui et al., 2026; , Koto et al., 2023; , Koto et al., 2020; , Liang et al., 2023).

Open questions and direction

We highlight the need for OCR-resilient ABSA-aware summarization pipelines that maintain citation-level fidelity and align outputs with primary ESG documents.
We advocate standardized, domain-specific evaluation suites that balance factual consistency, numerical accuracy, and evidence grounding with cross-source verification.
We urge integration of regulatory and rating-ecosystem signals (e.g., POJK, SPOTT/MSCI references) into the summarization and evaluation framework to support decision-making by investors and regulators.
