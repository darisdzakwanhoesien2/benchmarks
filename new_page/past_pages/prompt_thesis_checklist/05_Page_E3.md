E3. Paragraph-Level Logic

For each chapter, sample or examine the paragraphs and assess whether:

the opening sentence establishes the paragraph topic;
the paragraph develops one main issue, or at most two closely related issues;
evidence follows the claim;
citations are placed near the claims they support;
the final sentence provides closure or transition where appropriate;
multiple unrelated issues are not combined;
one argument is not unnecessarily fragmented into several very short paragraphs;
pronouns and references have clear antecedents;
transitions between paragraphs and sections are logical.

Classify problematic paragraphs as:

Missing topic sentence
Multiple unrelated topics
Unsupported claim
Weak evidence connection
Poor transition
Repetition
Paragraph too long
Paragraph too short
Conclusion appears before evidence
Citation scope unclear

For high-priority cases, propose a revised paragraph outline rather than merely correcting individual sentences.
