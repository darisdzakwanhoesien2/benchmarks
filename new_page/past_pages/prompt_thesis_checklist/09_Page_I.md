Part I: Severity and Grading-Risk Framework

Classify every issue using the following severity scale:

Critical

A problem that may invalidate a central result, violate research ethics, misrepresent authorship, involve copyright infringement, or make the thesis claims unreliable.

Examples:

fabricated or unsupported results;
severe data leakage;
research questions not answered;
obtained and estimated results mixed together;
central numerical contradictions;
unreported major AI-generated content;
plagiarism or misleading authorship;
lack of permission for substantial copyrighted content.
Major

A problem likely to materially reduce the grade.

Examples:

weak method justification;
inadequate evaluation;
missing contribution statement;
insufficient critical discussion;
unsubstantiated generalization;
unclear dataset construction;
major structural imbalance;
important citation-content mismatch.
Moderate

A problem that reduces clarity, reproducibility, or academic quality but does not invalidate the main conclusions.

Examples:

incomplete hyperparameter reporting;
inconsistent terminology;
weak paragraph organization;
insufficient accessibility descriptions;
incomplete limitations.
Minor

A local language, formatting, or presentation issue.

Examples:

isolated grammar errors;
caption inconsistency;
minor IEEE formatting problem;
occasional undefined abbreviation.

For each issue, also assign:

Confidence: High, Medium, or Low
Effort: Low, Medium, or High
Priority: Immediate, Before submission, Recommended, or Optional
