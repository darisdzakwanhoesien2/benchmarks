Taxonomy: End-to-end Pipeline for ESG/Climate Disclosure Analytics (Inputs: machine-readable PDFs, HTML, filings)

1) Ingestion and Versioning

Purpose
Establish a reproducible, auditable stream of governance-ready disclosures from diverse sources, with clear provenance and version history to support back-testing and regulatory inquiries (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Key design choices
Source federation: ingest corporate filings, sustainability reports, regulatory submissions, and partner datasets using a pluggable adapter layer; support both stable releases and live updates (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Versioned storage: immutable raw layer + mutable curated layer; store per-document metadata (source, year, language, standard, jurisdiction) and per-change lineage (diffs, retractions) (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Provenance tagging: attach document-level identifiers, section anchors, and page/section-level citations to every extracted unit to enable traceability (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Integrity checks: hash-based deduplication, schema validation, and anomaly detection on metadata (e.g., mismatched year, conflicting standards) to flag ingestion errors early (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Common failure modes
Missing or inconsistent metadata across sources (e.g., year, jurisdiction, standard) leading to misalignment in downstream stages (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Duplicate or near-duplicate filings causing fragmentation unless robust deduplication is applied (Mishra et al., 2024; .
Incomplete or corrupted source files (partial downloads, truncated PDFs) escaping initial checks (Mishra et al., 2024; .
Evaluation hooks
Ingestion completeness: coverage metrics (percent of target universe ingested per period) and timeliness (latency from release to ingestion) (Mishra et al., 2024; .
Provenance accuracy: randomized audit of source passages linked to extracted items; verify traceability chains end-to-end (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Metadata quality: precision/recall of critical fields (year, jurisdiction, standard) against a ground truth sample (Mishra et al., 2024; .
2) Text + Layout-aware Parsing

Purpose
Convert machine-readable inputs into structured representations that preserve document structure (sections, tables, footnotes) and visual/layout cues essential for correct interpretation Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Key design choices
Layout-aware extraction: use document structure signals (reading order, section headers, table captions, figure captions) to segment content into semantically meaningful units; preserve table geometry and hierarchical headings Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Multilingual support: enable language-aware parsing pipelines and keep language metadata for downstream normalization; leverage multilingual models and language-aware tokenization (Chen, 2025; Hang, 2025; Wang, 2025).
Rich metadata capture: store passage-level metadata (page, coordinates, font style) to support provenance and audit trails Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Modularity for modality; support both text-centric parsing and multimodal cues (tables/figures) when available Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Common failure modes
Breakage on irregular layouts (e.g., rotated tables, multi-column slides) causing mis-ordered text or collapsed table cells Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Loss of table semantics during extraction (merged cells, spanning headers) leading to incorrect entity anchoring Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Language混合 (code-switching) or mixed scripts causing segmentation errors if language handling is not robust Hang, 2025; Wang, 2025).
Evaluation hooks
Layout fidelity metrics: alignment of extracted sections, correct table row/column mappings, and preservation of captions with ground-truth layouts Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Table–text linkage accuracy: correctness of linking narrative statements to corresponding table entries and footnotes Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Multilingual parsing accuracy: language-detection accuracy and per-language extraction quality against labeled corpora (Chen, 2025; Hang, 2025; Wang, 2025).
3) Segmentation / Chunking

Purpose
Decompose documents into semantically coherent chunks (e.g., risk disclosures, targets, baselines, year-specific metrics) that are suitable for retrieval and extraction steps Khak et al., 2025)(Chen, 2025; .
Key design choices
Semantic unit boundaries: define chunking rules aligned with reporting standards (GHG Protocol scopes, KPI families, material topics) to ensure comparability across entities (Chen, 2025; Wei, 2025).
Proximity-aware chunking: group related passages (target + evidence) within the same section or table row, preserving contextual coherence and facilitating provenance tracing (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Flexible chunk granularity: support multiple granularity levels (sentence, clause, table cell, figure caption) to adapt to downstream extraction needs Khak et al., 2025)(Chen, 2025; .
Temporal tagging: annotate chunks with temporal anchors (year, horizon, baselines) to support longitudinal analyses (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Common failure modes
Over-segmentation that splits cohesive evidence across chunks, hampering cross-chunk reconciliation (Chen, 2025; .
Under-segmentation that bundles disparate topics (e.g., emissions and water risk) into a single chunk, confusing extraction models (Chen, 2025; .
Incorrect association of baselines or horizons due to ambiguous footnotes or table headers (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Evaluation hooks
Chunking accuracy: boundary precision/recall against expert-annotated partitions; measure boundary correctness for key units (targets, baselines) (Chen, 2025; .
Coherence metrics: intra-chunk semantic similarity and cross-chunk consistency checks with known reporting standards (Chen, 2025; .
Temporal consistency: verify that extracted temporal markers align with the document’s stated periods (Chen, 2025; .
4) Retrieval / Indexing

Purpose
Provide efficient access to corroborating passages, external standards, prior-year disclosures, and related documents to support evidence-based extraction and cross-document comparison Khak et al., 2025)Hang, 2025; Zhao et al., 2025).
Key design choices
Hybrid retrieval: combine vector-based semantic search with lexical/memory-based inverted indexing for exact-match essentials (identifiers, standard names) Khak et al., 2025)Zhao et al., 2025).
Contextual indexing: store passage-level provenance, chunk IDs, and cross-references (footnotes, appendices) to enable traceable retrieval and audit trails Khak et al., 2025)Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Evidence graphs: construct lightweight knowledge graphs linking entities, metrics, and documents to support multi-hop reasoning and explainability Zhao et al., 2025)(Mishra et al., 2024; .
Access control and freshness: enforce access controls where needed and version-indexed corpora reflecting regulatory updates and corrigenda (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Common failure modes
Stale or misaligned context: retrieved passages that do not match the current document’s scope or that refer to outdated standards Khak et al., 2025)(Chen, 2025; .
Retrieval bias: over-emphasis on frequently cited documents at the expense of newer or authoritative sources Khak et al., 2025)Zhao et al., 2025).
Incomplete evidence retrieval: missing corroborating passages due to fragmented indexing of footnotes or appendices Mishra et al., 2024; (Chen, 2025; .
Evaluation hooks
Retrieval precision/recall on a held-out set of target facts (e.g., boundary definitions, baseline years) linked to ground-truth passages Khak et al., 2025)(Chen, 2025; .
Evidence coverage metrics: fraction of extracted items with at least one retrieved corroborating passage and source citation Zhao et al., 2025).
Latency and throughput: end-to-end retrieval time per document and average time for multi-hop queries Khak et al., 2025)Zhao et al., 2025).
5) LLM Extraction

Purpose
Detect, normalize, and extract ESG/climate entities, metrics, targets, baselines, time horizons, and qualitative statements from the segmented content; produce structured representations with provenance Khak et al., 2025)(Ni et al., 2023; Zitu et al., 2025; .
Key design choices
Prompt design with provenance: craft prompts that instruct the model to output structured records while attaching source passages and confidence estimates; include explicit temporal and boundary qualifiers Khak et al., 2025)(Ni et al., 2023; Zitu et al., 2025; .
Normalization discipline: map diverse expressions to canonical entities (e.g., “Scope 1+2 emissions” → standardized scope label; “tCO2e” → metric unit) and standardize date formats (Chen, 2025; Wei, 2025).
Calibration and confidence: require the model to provide calibrated confidence levels and to cite retrieved passages for each assertion Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
Human-in-the-loop gates: implement review checkpoints for high-stakes extractions (e.g., targets, baselines, verifications) to mitigate hallucinations Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; Schmoll, 2025).
Common failure modes
Hallucinations: LLMs may state unsupported facts or misstate scopes/years without adequate provenance Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
Inconsistent extractions over time or across similar documents due to prompt sensitivity or model drift Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
Ambiguity in euphemistic or hedged language leading to misclassification of targets versus performance (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Evaluation hooks
Extraction accuracy: precision/recall/F1 for entities, targets, baselines, and time horizons against a gold standard; assess entity-level calibration Zitu et al., 2025; Schmoll, 2025).
Provenance fidelity: proportion of extractions with correct supporting passages and citations; auditability score Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
Cross-document consistency: agreement of similar extractions across related documents and years; measure variance under controlled prompts Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
6) Normalization / Entity Resolution

Purpose
Reconcile heterogeneous representations into a unified, ontology-aligned schema; resolve synonymous entities, standardize organizations, standards, and metric units; enable cross-document comparability (Chen, 2025; Wei, 2025).
Key design choices
Ontology-driven mapping: align to ESG reporting ontologies (e.g., GRI, SASB/IFRS S1/S2 concepts) and domain-specific taxonomies for emissions scopes, KPIs, and baselines (Chen, 2025; Wei, 2025).
Cross-document entity resolution: fuzzy matching with contextual features (location, date, table header) to link mentions across documents to the same entity or concept (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Unit and scope normalization: convert disparate units to canonical units (e.g., tCO2e), harmonize scopes (Scope 1/2/3), and align baselines and time horizons (Chen, 2025; Wei, 2025).
Confidence logging: attach confidence scores to resolved entities and maintain provenance trails for auditability (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Common failure modes
Incorrect disambiguation of similar company names or subsidiaries across jurisdictions; misattribution of metrics (Chen, 2025; .
Inconsistent scope mappings leading to erroneous comparability (e.g., Scope 2 vs. Market-based Scope 2) (Chen, 2025; .
Loss of nuance when collapsing hedged language into crisp labels (e.g., “estimated” or “could” under- or over-states certainty) (Chen, 2025; .
Evaluation hooks
Entity resolution accuracy: precision/recall for canonicalized entities and metrics; cross-check with ground-truth alignments (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Consistency checks: percentage of cross-document concordant mappings and detection of conflicting resolutions (Chen, 2025; .
Provenance integrity: verify resolved items retain traceability to original passages and metadata Cui, 2025; .
7) Knowledge Representation / Lineage

Purpose
Store extracted facts in a structured, queryable representation that encodes provenance, temporal context, and document lineage; enable auditability, explainability, and regulatory reporting (Ni et al., 2023; Hang, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Key design choices
Provenance-rich graph or frame store: use a knowledge-graph or property graph to capture relationships among entities, targets, evidence passages, and standards; capture source, page, and section anchors for each claim Zhao et al., 2025)(Mishra et al., 2024; .
Temporal lineage: attach time indices, revision history, and versioned snapshots to enable backtesting and change detection over time (Ni et al., 2023; Hang, 2025; (Chen, 2025; .
Queryable schema: design flexible schemas allowing common queries (e.g., “emissions target by company by year under GHG Protocol scope 1+2”) and support for aggregation, filtration by jurisdiction, and standardization levels (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Compliance and explainability: store alternative interpretations, confidence, and rationales to support human review and regulatory disclosure needs Khak et al., 2025)Zitu et al., 2025; .
Common failure modes
Provenance drift: mismatches between extracted facts and their recorded sources due to incomplete lineage capture (Chen, 2025; .
Schema drift: evolving taxonomy causing misalignment of legacy records with new standards; fragmentation across versions (Ni et al., 2023; (Chen, 2025; .
Scalability bottlenecks: graph growth and query latency as the corpus expands; performance degradation without careful indexing Zhao et al., 2025).
Evaluation hooks
Lineage correctness: end-to-end verification that each fact traces precisely to the cited source, with reproducible reconstruction paths (Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; .
Query performance: latency, throughput, and correctness of typical queries; index hit rates and cache effectiveness Zhao et al., 2025).
Auditability audits: periodic checks by domain experts comparing representations against ground-truth documents; measure time-to-audit and error rates (Ni et al., 2023; Zitu et al., 2025; .
Notes on scope and integration

The taxonomy above intentionally centers on digital-text inputs (machine-readable PDFs, HTML, filings) and explicitly excludes OCR-only or image-based pipelines as a scope constraint; OCR-centric processing is out of scope to emphasize layout-aware, text-first architectures and provenance-rich analytics (Mishra et al., 2024; Atagong et al., 2025).
Inter-stage dependencies are designed for near-term deployment with commonly available models and tools, emphasizing traceability, modularity, and governance to support robust ESG/climate analytics in practice Khak et al., 2025)(Chen, 2025; Cui, 2025; (Mishra et al., 2024; .
Running exemplars for the taxonomy

Text/Layout Parsing: the emissions table is parsed with row/column alignment preserved; footnotes about baselines are captured as separate provenance entries.
Segmentation/Chunking: the target, baseline, and horizon are segmented into a single target chunk with linked evidence chunks (prior-year disclosure and external standard).
Retrieval/Indexing: corroborating passages (earlier reports, regulatory guidance) are retrieved and indexed against the target chunk for multi-hop reasoning.
LLM Extraction: the target sentence is extracted as a structured record with fields for target, scope, baseline, year, unit, and confidence, plus links to provenance.
Normalization/Entity Resolution: the company, scope definitions, and units are normalized to a canonical schema; identical targets across years are linked.
Knowledge Representation/Lineage: the final fact is stored in a provenance-rich graph with links to the source passages, versioned snapshots, and audit trail notes.
