The real problems — it is not just total n

n=272 is the symptom. These four structural issues are the actual diagnosis, and each has a different fix.

Problem 1 — Few-shot template is severely underpowered
n=14 · power=43%
The few-shot Indonesian template produced only 14 records across a single document. Power for detecting the CoT vs zero-shot difference is 43% — meaning there is a 57% chance you would miss a real effect even if it exists. No comparative claim is valid for this template.

The few-shot result (balanced commitment=35.7% and outcome=35.7%) is actually the most interesting finding — but it is statistically invisible at n=14.
→ Fix: Run 2–3 more few-shot batches on different documents. Adds ~25–40 records. Takes 2 hours. Brings power to 86–95%.
Problem 2 — Only 6 documents for greenwashing index
6 docs · 1 failed
The greenwashing index computed across 5 working documents is a case study comparison, not a validated metric. You cannot determine whether the domestic/international GW difference (+0.500 gap) is a general pattern or document-specific variation without ≥15–20 documents. Any reader will note this immediately.

This is the most important limitation for the thesis's central contribution.
→ Fix: Add 9–14 more Indonesian sustainability reports from IDX. Each adds ~8–12 records per run. Total effort: 2–3 days compute.
Problem 3 — S-pillar has only 4 records
n=4 · zero value
Social pillar at 1.2% of records means zero analytical power. This is page-selection bias: your extraction batches focused on environmental and governance report sections. The overall n=272 is completely irrelevant to this problem — the subgroup n is what matters.

Any ESG ABSA study that cannot discuss the Social pillar is fundamentally incomplete.
→ Fix: Select social-topic page batches from PTBA (has CSR sections) and VKTR (community pages). Re-run with Arcee + CoT-English. Takes 1 day, adds ~30–40 S-pillar records.
Problem 4 — Cross-model comparison is fully confounded
0 shared inputs
Arcee Trinity and GPT-oss-120b never processed the same document with the same prompt. Arcee ran on all 6 documents with 5 prompts. GPT-oss ran on 1 document with 1 (broken) prompt. There is no valid cross-model comparison possible — it is not an imbalance problem, it is a complete confound.

This means RQ6's cross-model claim cannot be answered with current data at all.
→ Fix: Re-run GPT-oss on PTBA and ICR using ZS-English and CoT-English prompts. Takes 1–2 days. Produces ~40–60 matched records for Cohen's κ computation.
Direct answer to the question "what is the suitable number and why":

The answer has two parts depending on what you are claiming:

For a master's thesis feasibility study (your current frame): The minimum credible target is n=720 from ≥12 documents. This achieves the full pillar×language×tone subgroup matrix (30 per cell), reduces worst-case MoE to ±3.6pp, and gives ≥40 per prompt template. You need approximately 450 more records — about 53 more extraction runs at your current rate, achievable in 3–4 days of compute.

For a study making comparative or generalisable claims: The minimum is n=1,000 from ≥15 documents, matching the lower end of comparable published ESG NLP studies (Moreno & Caminero 2020, Garrido-Merchan 2023). This is still well within reach without new data sources — the IDX sustainability disclosure portal has hundreds of publicly available reports.

The number you should NOT try to reach: n=2,000+ for fine-tuning. That requires expert annotation at scale and is PhD-level work. Your thesis does not need it.
